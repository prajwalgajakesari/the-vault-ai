# Stripe Is in Talks to Buy AI Model Marketplace OpenRouter for About $10 Billion

Stripe, the payments giant that quietly runs the financial plumbing behind much of the internet, is in advanced talks to acquire OpenRouter, the buzzy marketplace that lets developers route requests across hundreds of AI models — a deal that could value the three-year-old startup at roughly $10 billion, according to a report published July 23 by The Wall Street Journal.

The price is arresting. OpenRouter was valued at just $1.3 billion in May, when it raised a $113 million Series B led by CapitalG, Google's growth fund. A $10 billion sale would mark a nearly eightfold markup in under three months — a multiple that says as much about where investors now believe AI value is settling as it does about any single company. A final agreement could be announced soon, people familiar with the talks told the Journal, though negotiations remain fluid, could collapse, or draw a competing bidder. Several large technology firms have reportedly kicked the tires.

For Stripe, valued at $159 billion earlier this year, the acquisition would rank among its boldest moves beyond payments and deepen a fast-accelerating push into AI infrastructure. The two companies are already entwined: OpenRouter uses Stripe Invoicing, Stripe Tax, Stripe's fraud tools, and Stripe's rails to accept everything from credit cards to Alipay. "Stripe already runs a significant share of OpenRouter's financial operations behind the scenes," PYMNTS noted, framing a full acquisition as "a natural next step."

## What OpenRouter Actually Does

OpenRouter builds no models of its own. It sits in the middle of the AI stack, giving developers a single API to reach more than 400 models from over 60 providers — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, DeepSeek — and switch between them without rewriting their software. It picks which model should answer each request based on price, speed, and reliability, then falls back to another provider when one goes down.

That positioning has made it indispensable at startling speed. The company now processes an estimated 1.5 quadrillion tokens a year — a one followed by 15 zeros — serving more than 8 million developers, according to Menlo Ventures, an early backer. By Menlo's math, that volume equals 15% to 30% of Google's total token traffic and more than half of Microsoft's Azure AI Foundry. AI researcher Andrej Karpathy has called OpenRouter the de facto "transfer switch" of AI. Menlo partners Deedy Das and Matt Murphy went further: "We think they're going to be much more than just a 'transfer switch'; it could be the entire power grid."

Remarkably, OpenRouter charges no markup over what the underlying model providers already charge. It makes money on a small fee when developers top up their accounts. Sacra estimates that business hit roughly $50 million in annualized revenue by March, up from about $19 million at the end of 2025. With around 50 employees, that works out to some $2 million in net revenue per head. The company was co-founded in 2023 by Alex Atallah, a three-time founder who previously built the $13 billion NFT marketplace OpenSea, alongside COO Chris Clark.

## Why It Matters

The deal is the clearest sign yet that the AI economy's value is migrating from raw model-building toward the connective tissue that meters, routes, and bills for intelligence. It would be Stripe's second AI acquisition in under a year, following its January purchase of Metronome, a usage-based billing engine whose customers include OpenAI, Anthropic, and Nvidia.

The two fit together with almost suspicious neatness. OpenRouter decides which model handles a request; Metronome measures what that request cost and turns it into a bill. Bolt Stripe's payment rails onto both, and you have an end-to-end system for the coming agent economy — one where autonomous software, not humans, initiates the vast majority of transactions. Stripe co-founder and CEO Patrick Collison has argued exactly that future is near. Owning the layer that chooses the model, meters the usage, and settles the payment would let Stripe sit at the toll booth of AI consumption, capturing distribution and billing infrastructure created by enterprises' multi-model strategies. It also marks a consolidation moment: the AI middleware that startups scrambled to build is now being absorbed by incumbents with the balance sheets to own the whole rail.

## What to Watch

Whether the deal closes at all is the first question — the Journal stresses talks are unfinished, and rival suitors could emerge for an asset this strategically located. Watch the final valuation and structure: an ~8x markup in three months will test how founders, and CapitalG, value staying independent. Watch, too, for neutrality concerns — OpenRouter's appeal rests on treating every model provider evenhandedly, and a Stripe (or CapitalG-adjacent) owner could raise questions among the AI labs it routes to. Finally, watch regulators and rivals: if Stripe locks up model routing, usage metering, and payments, expect competitors to race to assemble an alternative stack before the toll booth is built.
