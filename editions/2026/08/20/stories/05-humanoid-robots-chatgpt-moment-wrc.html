Unitree's CEO Says Robots Are Nearing Their ChatGPT Moment. The Shipment Numbers Are Already Moving.

5 min read 1004 words 4 sources
KEY TAKEAWAY

Humanoid shipments nearly quadrupled to 19,100 units in H1 2026 with China at 97% share, but Wang Xingxing's own timeline for the ChatGPT moment stretches to ten years and rival shipment counts differ by more than 2x.

At a booth in Beijing this week, a humanoid torso mounted on a wheeled tripod picked through parcels — the same job it has been doing at China Post logistics sites since last year. A few aisles over, robots from a startup called DexForce packed mobile phones into boxes on a mock assembly line, correcting for handsets placed at an angle. The 2026 World Robot Conference, which opened Wednesday and runs through Sunday, was built around one argument: that Chinese humanoids have graduated from kung-fu demos to timesheets.

The industry's best-known founder made that argument in the loftiest terms available.

'We are marching towards a ChatGPT moment in embodied intelligence,' Wang Xingxing, founder and chief executive of Unitree Robotics, told the conference on Thursday, according to Reuters. He defined the milestone with unusual precision: 'We hope that in the future, we can see a robot be introduced into an unfamiliar household and it can achieve approximately 80% of tasks successfully through voice or text commands.' That, he said, would be 'an important tipping point for the robot industry to usher in explosive growth.'

Then he spent much of the same appearance explaining why it has not happened yet.

The shipment numbers are moving

The commercial backdrop is genuinely striking. Global humanoid robot shipments reached roughly 19,100 units in the first half of 2026, up 272% from about 5,100 a year earlier, according to Smart Analytics Global, a California-based research firm. Chinese vendors accounted for more than 97% of those shipments; Chinese customers made up more than 85% of demand.

The leaderboard also shifted. Shanghai-based AGIBOT shipped roughly 8,400 units for a 44% global share, overtaking Unitree, which shipped about 5,900 for 31%. Between them, the two firms moved about three-quarters of the world's humanoids. SAG expects full-year 2026 shipments to approach 60,000 units on industry revenue of about 1.6 billion US dollars, reaching 500,000 units by 2030.

The mix matters more than the total. Industrial and commercial applications represented more than 70% of first-half shipments, against roughly 50% a year earlier. 'Global humanoid robot shipments nearly quadrupled in 1H 2026, but an equally important shift is taking place in how these robots are being deployed,' said Linda Sui, founder and principal at SAG.

That shift was visible on the Beijing floor. More than 300 companies, mostly domestic, showed over 2,000 exhibits and launched more than 150 products, organisers said. Robotera said it had over 100 parcel-sorting robots running in 15 warehouses nationwide. DexForce said its machines have been deployed since early this year at a factory run by Lens Technology, a touchscreen supplier to Apple and Huawei. 'Our most common application scenarios are in logistics,' said Zhang Dapeng, an assistant vice president at industrial humanoid firm Leju.

Analysis: three reasons to hold the ChatGPT comparison loosely

First, the man making the claim keeps undercutting it. Wang told the conference a major leap in robot software could arrive in two to three years optimistically — or in five to ten years at the latest. He said humanoids are not yet capable enough for mass deployment, called AI decision-making the industry's biggest bottleneck, and conceded Unitree is 'lagging behind' in real-world application of physical AI models. 'Relative to the mood around World Robot Conference and Unitree's spectacular IPO, Wang Xingxing was notably sober about current capabilities,' said Georg Stieler, head of automation at the robotics consultancy Stieler. A ChatGPT moment that might be a decade out is not a moment. It is a roadmap.

Second, nobody agrees on what is being counted. On the same day Wang spoke, a Chinese humanoid industry body reported that China alone delivered more than 40,000 humanoids in the first half — more than double SAG's estimate for the entire world. Both figures cannot be right. The definitions of 'humanoid' and of 'shipment' are doing enormous, unaudited work here, and a sector this politically favoured has every incentive to count generously.

Third, a shipped robot is not a working robot. Unitree's own IPO prospectus shows most of its customers are universities and research institutions — buyers of platforms, not labour. Where humanoids are genuinely deployed, the economics are still marginal: Robotera's parcel sorters run 900 to 1,000 hours continuously and are about 85% as effective as a human worker. Unitree's machines must be retrained largely from scratch for each new task, and that failure to generalise is precisely the wall the whole industry is hitting. X Square Robot, which focuses on household chores, said its longest deployment has lasted one month.

The analogy itself is also load-bearing in a way it should not be. ChatGPT scaled because software distribution is nearly free. Humanoids carry a bill of materials, a service contract, a safety case and a depreciation schedule. The full-year forecast from SAG implies an average selling price near 27,000 dollars, and no world model makes that cheaper than a warehouse worker.

Markets are already registering the ambivalence. Unitree's Shanghai-listed shares fell 11% on Thursday, a day after soaring nearly sixfold in their trading debut.

What to watch next

Three checkpoints. Whether the industrial and commercial share of shipments keeps climbing past 70% in second-half data — that ratio, not the headline unit count, is the real commercialisation signal. Whether X Square's move from one-month pilots to hundreds of homes this year survives contact with actual households. And 2028, the date Galbot founder Wang He put on the record: 'With continued accumulation of data and further technological breakthroughs, we expect to reach the ChatGPT moment for embodied intelligence by 2028,' defining it as robots handling 70% to 80% of everyday tasks without specialised training.

That is a falsifiable claim with a deadline, which makes it more useful than most of what was said in Beijing. Meanwhile, the US Federal Communications Commission last month banned future imports of foreign-made humanoid and quadruped robots on national security grounds — meaning the country shipping 97% of the world's humanoids is increasingly locked out of the market most eager to debate them.
