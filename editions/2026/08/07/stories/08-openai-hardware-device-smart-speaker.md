# OpenAI's First Hardware Device Takes Shape: A Screenless, Puck-Sized Smart Speaker

After more than a year of secrecy, the shape of OpenAI's first consumer gadget is finally coming into focus, and it looks less like a phone than a piece of furniture that talks back. According to a Bloomberg report cited across the tech press this week, the ChatGPT maker is building a screenless smart speaker roughly the size of a hockey puck, doughnut-shaped and battery-powered, designed to be scooped up in one hand and carried from room to room. It is expected to ship in 2027 and to cost more than $300.

The device is the first tangible product to emerge from OpenAI's headline-grabbing partnership with Jony Ive, the former Apple design chief whose studio LoveFrom is co-designing the hardware. OpenAI acquired Ive's device startup, io, in a deal valued at roughly $6.5 billion, folding the team into a new hardware division. This puck is what that money has been buying.

## A Speaker Designed to Have a "Personality"

What separates the OpenAI device from the Echos and HomePods already sitting on kitchen counters is not just the absence of a screen. According to Bloomberg's Mark Gurman, the defining feature is its "personality" and its ability to form a connection with the person using it. The gadget is reported to include moving parts that shift to signal when it is responding or paying attention, along with lights that indicate when it is listening, plus a camera, an array of microphones and environmental sensors.

The physical concept is deliberately ambient. Reports describe a battery-powered object meant to be held, set down on a surface or moved around the home, working in different positions rather than staying tethered to a single spot. Upscale materials, including high-quality metal, are said to be part of the plan, consistent with Ive's design sensibility and the premium price point.

The ambitions go beyond answering trivia questions. In an internal presentation, OpenAI employees were reportedly told the speaker would observe users and proactively suggest actions to help them meet their goals, such as recommending an earlier bedtime ahead of a morning meeting. The camera is designed to help the device learn about who is using it and what surrounds them, with reporting indicating a facial-recognition capability comparable to Face ID and the ability to make purchases by voice. Former Apple designer Evans Hankey is said to be leading industrial design, with Ive making the final call on nearly all design decisions.

## The Vision Behind the Hardware Push

The device represents OpenAI's most concrete step into consumer hardware, a market Sam Altman has openly coveted. When Altman and Ive first announced their partnership alongside the io acquisition, Altman framed the mission as "figuring out how to create a family of devices that would let people use AI to create all sorts of wonderful things." The puck-shaped speaker appears to be the first member of that family, with AI glasses and a pin among the products rumored to follow.

Altman has described the forthcoming hardware as something more "peaceful and calm" than the smartphone, an implicit critique of the attention-grabbing screens that dominate modern life. A screenless, voice-first speaker fits that framing, positioning ChatGPT's advanced voice mode as an always-available presence in the home rather than an app buried behind a lock screen.

## Why It Matters

The bet here is that the next major computing platform after the smartphone will be ambient, driven by voice and context rather than taps and swipes. Smart speakers have existed for a decade, but Amazon's Alexa, Apple's Siri and Google Assistant largely stalled as command-and-control tools that set timers and play music. OpenAI is wagering that a genuinely conversational large language model, wrapped in Ive-caliber industrial design, can turn the category into something people bond with rather than bark orders at.

That collides directly with the incumbents who own the living room. At $300 to $400, OpenAI's device would sit at the premium end of a crowded shelf. Amazon's Echo line runs from roughly $40 to $240, while Apple's HomePod starts at $299. Apple, Amazon and Google all have the advantage of installed hardware bases, existing accounts and cheaper price points, and each is racing to graft generative AI onto its assistant. OpenAI has the more advanced conversational model but no living-room foothold, no music service and no smart-home ecosystem to lean on.

The Ive collaboration is the wildcard. His design pedigree gives OpenAI a credibility in physical products that no other AI lab can claim, and the io deal signaled that Altman views hardware as strategically essential rather than a side project. The partnership has not been without friction. Apple sued OpenAI in July over the alleged misappropriation of hardware trade secrets, and OpenAI moved to dismiss the suit this week, a reminder that the boundary between the two companies runs through former Apple employees now building competing devices.

## What to Watch

The most important caveat is timing. A 2027 ship date is roughly 18 months out, and hardware projects of this complexity, especially first-generation ones with moving parts and cameras, slip. Watch for whether OpenAI locks in a manufacturing partner and firms up the price. Reports have already tied the effort to suppliers experienced in precision assembly, and pricing between $300 and $400 will be tested against consumer appetite for a premium, screenless AI companion.

The bigger questions are about trust. A device with an always-listening microphone, an environmental camera and facial recognition, one that suggests when you should go to bed and can make purchases on your behalf, invites obvious privacy scrutiny. Whether buyers welcome such a machine into their homes, and whether OpenAI can convince them it belongs there, will decide if this puck becomes the iPhone of ambient AI or the next well-designed gadget that never finds its audience.
