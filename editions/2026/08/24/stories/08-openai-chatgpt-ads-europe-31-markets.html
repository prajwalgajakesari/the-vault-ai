ChatGPT Ads Went Live in 31 European Markets. Free and Go Users Are the Product.

5 min read 1010 words 4 sources
KEY TAKEAWAY

OpenAI turned on advertising for ChatGPT Free and Go users across 31 European markets, running contextual ads under GDPR 'legitimate interest' and personalized ads on opt-in consent — a consent-or-pay structure Irish regulators have not yet blessed.

As of this morning, a person in Munich or Milan asking ChatGPT which laptop to buy may get a sponsored answer alongside the real one. OpenAI's advertising business went live today across 31 European markets — the 27 EU member states plus Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Switzerland — in the largest single geographic expansion the six-month-old ad product has attempted. It takes ChatGPT Ads from nine countries to roughly 40 in one step.

The mechanics are unchanged from the U.S. pilot that started in February 2026. Ads appear only on the Free and Go tiers. Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Education stay ad-free. Placements are labeled and rendered separately from the model's response; advertisers get aggregate view and click counts, not conversations. In Europe, OpenAI has also excluded accounts it predicts belong to minors — a protection it did not announce in the U.S.

What is different is the legal plumbing underneath, and that is the actual story. OpenAI is converting a consumer subscription company into an advertising company, and it has chosen to do the hard part of that conversion in the jurisdiction with the world's strictest rules on exactly this.

Who is buying, and how

There is no self-serve buying in Europe yet. From today, campaigns run through OpenAI's Ads Solutions team, technology partners, and the six holding companies and agency groups named at launch: Publicis, Omnicom, WPP, Havas, Dentsu and MediaPlus. Self-serve access through Ads Manager is promised "later this summer," which leaves OpenAI roughly four weeks of summer to ship it. The U.S. self-serve tool debuted this spring with a $50,000 minimum spend that has since been dropped.

The commercial case is not subtle. OpenAI's ad revenue has climbed more than 25% since the start of August, enterprise CMO Colin Fleming told reporters, and the business is nearing a $1 billion annualized run rate. Fleming pegged ChatGPT at one billion weekly active users, about 20% of whose queries show commercial intent. Against a company that has told investors to expect $100 billion in revenue within four years — a mark Meta took 17 years to hit — monetizing the free tier is not a side project.

Agency enthusiasm is real but hedged. "Europe makes natural sense for OpenAI to tap into in order to expand their ad revenue footprint," said Shamsul Chowdhury, svp of paid media at Zeno Group. "The big watchout will be how AI is policed in EMEA." Victor Batista, senior director of paid media at Jellyfish, whose clients were in the pilot, put the product's maturity plainly to Digiday: "This is very much unproven. This is very new, and they are building at a very fast pace."

OpenAI's own framing, from vp of ads Dave Dugan: "Europe is home to some of the world's most creative and sophisticated advertising markets, where expectations around relevance, quality and trust are rightly high. We look forward to meeting that bar."

What GDPR actually requires here

OpenAI's announcement leans on the word "consent," but the European system has two layers with two different legal bases, and only one of them involves asking.

At launch, the ads Europeans see are contextual: matched to the current conversation topic, approximate city-level location, device type, time of day and language. Past chats and stored memories are excluded. That processing runs under GDPR Article 6(1)(f) — legitimate interest — with no permission requested. The second layer, personalized ads drawing on ad-interaction history, inferred interests and advertiser conversion data, requires an explicit opt-in that can be withdrawn in account settings.

This inverts the path Meta was dragged down. Meta argued behavioral ads were part of its user contract, lost, argued legitimate interest, lost again, and was fined €390 million in January 2023 before consent became its only remaining option. OpenAI starts where Meta ended, reserving legitimate interest for the narrow session-level targeting regulators have historically tolerated.

The unresolved question is whether the whole arrangement clears the bar for consent being "freely given." Go costs €8 a month and shows ads. Plus costs €23 and does not. A no-ads free tier exists but strips daily message limits, image generation and deep research. "The fact it only applies to users on the more basic plans, which are free, could effectively result in users feeling pressured to go onto a paid plan," said Clara Westbrook, head of data privacy at Arbor Law. "It almost feels like people are having to pay if they don't want to receive ads at all."

That is precisely the terrain of EDPB Opinion 08/2024 on consent-or-pay models. The Irish Data Protection Commission, lead supervisory authority for OpenAI Ireland Limited, has said nothing publicly about the rollout or the August 14 EU privacy policy it rests on.

The incentive problem nobody has solved

Every safeguard OpenAI cites is genuine: separate infrastructure, no advertiser access to chats, no paying to influence answers. The objection is not that these controls are fake. It is that they are voluntary, and the company maintaining them is building a revenue engine whose growth depends on eroding them at the margin. Former OpenAI research scientist Zoë Hitzig resigned the day the U.S. ad system launched in February, calling it "the Facebook lesson" — incentives accumulate even when today's commitments are sincere.

A search engine that ranks a sponsored result badly loses a click. An assistant that shades a recommendation loses nothing visible, because there is no organic ranking to compare it against. You cannot audit an answer you only see once.

What to watch

Three things. Whether the Irish DPC or any national authority opens an inquiry into the consent-or-pay structure. Whether self-serve Ads Manager lands before September, which would signal how much regulatory friction OpenAI is really absorbing. And whether the personalization opt-in rate is ever disclosed — if it is low, pressure to reclassify more targeting as "contextual" arrives fast. The EU AI Act's fining powers went live on August 2, three weeks before this launch. Europe is not just the biggest new market for ChatGPT Ads. It is the stress test.
