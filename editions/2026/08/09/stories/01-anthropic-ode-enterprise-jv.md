# Anthropic Launches "Ode With Anthropic," a $1.5B Enterprise Joint Venture With Blackstone and Hellman & Friedman

Anthropic is making its biggest bet yet that the money in artificial intelligence lies not only in building frontier models, but in the far messier work of getting them to actually run inside regulated businesses. The company has stood up **Ode with Anthropic**, a $1.5 billion enterprise AI services firm launched in partnership with private-equity heavyweights **Blackstone** and **Hellman & Friedman**. As of this week the joint venture is operational, with roughly **100 engineers** on the ground and a mandate to plant Claude deep inside industries that have historically been slow, and cautious, adopters of new technology.

The venture is structured as a standalone company rather than a business unit, a deliberate choice that lets it recruit, price and deploy on its own terms while drawing on Anthropic's models. Beyond the three founding partners, Ode arrives backed by an unusually deep investor consortium: **Goldman Sachs, General Atlantic, Apollo Global Management, GIC, Sequoia Capital**, and Leonard Green & Partners. That roster reads less like a typical AI cap table and more like a who's-who of the firms that finance and own the mid-market economy Ode is now hunting for customers in.

## The Target: The Unglamorous Middle

Ode's pitch is aimed squarely at the companies that dominate the economy but rarely make AI headlines: mid-sized banks, regional health systems, and manufacturers. These are organizations that want the productivity gains everyone is promising from generative AI but lack the in-house machine-learning teams to build anything themselves, and cannot afford the compliance risk of getting it wrong. Ode's proposition is to deploy Claude in what the partners describe as regulated and sovereign, compliance-ready configurations, then stay to build the systems around it.

The firm is built on real operational bones rather than a fresh org chart. Ode grew out of **Fractional AI**, an applied AI services company Anthropic acquired in May 2026, and is led by Fractional co-founders **Chris Taylor** as chief executive and **Eddie Siegel** as chief technology officer. Their team, folded together with engineers seconded from Anthropic, forms the operational core now going to market. The involvement of Blackstone and Hellman & Friedman is not incidental: both firms own sprawling portfolios of exactly the kind of mid-cap companies Ode wants as clients, giving the venture a built-in pipeline of potential deployments across finance, healthcare, and industrial businesses.

The ambition, at least as Ode's leadership frames it, is enormous. "It's pretty easy to imagine this as a trillion-dollar company someday if we execute well," Taylor told TechCrunch, a line that captures both the confidence and the still-unproven nature of the enterprise. For now, the reality is 100 engineers and a thesis that implementation, not raw model capability, is the bottleneck standing between AI's promise and its payoff in the real economy.

## Why It Matters

Ode reframes a debate that has quietly split the AI industry: is the durable value in the models, or in everything that surrounds them? Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google have poured tens of billions into training ever-larger systems, but frontier models are increasingly commoditized and interchangeable. The harder, stickier problem is integration, data plumbing, security review, regulatory sign-off, and change management inside organizations that cannot simply pipe customer data into a chatbot. By spinning up a dedicated services firm, Anthropic is betting that the next trillion-dollar layer of the AI economy is implementation, the consulting-and-deployment work that has traditionally belonged to the Accentures and Deloittes of the world.

That is a notable strategic divergence. OpenAI has leaned on Microsoft's enterprise sales machine and a growing partner network; Anthropic is instead building a captive, jointly owned deployment arm with private equity firms that bring both capital and customers. It is also a hedge. Model revenue is volatile and price-competitive; services revenue is recurring, relationship-driven, and far harder for a rival to dislodge once Claude is embedded in a bank's compliance workflow or a hospital system's back office.

The timing is pointed. Ode's launch lands the same week Anthropic named **Mariano-Florentino (Tino) Cuéllar**, a former California Supreme Court justice and past president of the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace, as its first **Chief Global Affairs Officer**. Cuéllar's appointment, reporting to president Daniela Amodei, signals how seriously Anthropic is taking the regulatory and geopolitical dimensions of enterprise and government AI, precisely the terrain Ode must navigate to sell into regulated and sovereign environments. A company chasing banks and health systems needs credibility on compliance and policy as much as it needs good engineers.

## What to Watch

The open question is execution, the word Taylor himself hung the trillion-dollar dream on. A 100-engineer firm cannot serve thousands of mid-market clients directly, so the near-term test is whether Ode can productize its deployments, turning bespoke integrations into repeatable, compliance-ready templates that scale. Watch, too, for channel conflict: Ode competes with the same systems integrators and consultancies that Anthropic partners with elsewhere, and with in-house AI teams at larger enterprises.

Longer term, the venture is a live experiment in whether a model lab can also be a services company without diluting either business. If Blackstone and Hellman & Friedman start routing their portfolio companies through Ode at scale, the joint venture could become one of the fastest paths to enterprise revenue in the industry. If regulated clients balk at the pace, the cost, or the risk, it will stand as a cautionary tale about the gap between AI's demo and its deployment. Either way, Ode has made the industry's implicit bet explicit: the hardest, and perhaps most valuable, part of AI is not building it, but making it work.
