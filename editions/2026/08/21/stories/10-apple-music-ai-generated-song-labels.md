Apple has spent five months letting the music industry decide, quietly, whether to admit a song was made by a machine. That grace period is ending. In an email to Apple Music content partners this week, the company said it will begin showing a "Made With AI" label — visible to every subscriber — on any track a content provider has flagged as "materially generated using AI." The labels arrive "later this year," per the email, first reported by The Hollywood Reporter and Billboard on Thursday, August 20.

The change converts a back-office metadata field into a consumer-facing badge, and an optional courtesy into a requirement.

## From Optional Metadata to a Visible Badge

Apple introduced AI Transparency Tags in March 2026, a disclosure system covering four assets: artwork, track, composition (lyrics), and music video. At launch the tags were voluntary, with Apple deferring to content providers to decide what counted as AI-generated, and the resulting data lived largely out of listeners' view.

This week's email hardens both halves of that arrangement. Apple now says the tags are required in "any instance where AI was used to create a material portion of the content, including tracks that are AI platform generated." It defines AI-platform-generated content as "anything that is primarily derived from a generative AI service" — language that sweeps in output from tools like Suno and Udio while leaving room for the middle ground of AI-assisted mixing, mastering, and stem separation that studios already use daily.

Apple gave no launch date beyond "later this year," and did not detail enforcement: what happens to a provider that systematically under-tags, or whether listeners get a filter to suppress tagged tracks.

Apple Music vice president Oliver Schusser told Billboard in April 2026 that more than a third of monthly uploads to Apple Music are 100% AI-generated — while AI music accounted for less than 0.5% of actual listening. Schusser said Apple operates an in-house AI detection system but still relies on providers to disclose. That gap — between what Apple can detect and what it will act on — is the crux of the policy.

## Deezer Got Here First, With Numbers

Apple is not first. Deezer built a proprietary AI detection tool in January 2025, began tagging AI music for listeners in June 2025 — the first streaming service to do so — and has published upload statistics since. On July 21, 2026, Deezer reported that AI-generated uploads had crossed a threshold: roughly 90,000 fully AI-generated tracks per day, more than 50% of all new daily music deliveries at peak in June 2026 — up from about 75,000 per day (44% of uploads) in April 2026, 60,000 in January 2026, and just 10,000 in January 2025. Deezer tagged over 13.4 million AI-generated tracks in 2025 alone.

"Deezer has been at the frontline of fighting fraud and reducing payment dilution related to AI music for almost two years. Now that half of all daily uploads are AI-generated tracks, we are taking additional steps to safeguard the rights of artists and songwriters, while maintaining focus on music that fans actually love," said Alexis Lanternier, CEO of Deezer, in the company's July 21 announcement. "We have reached a pivotal moment, but we have the technology to keep track of the development, and an ambition to do what's right. If the industry can align and come together to create shared standards, we can build a healthy and sustainable music ecosystem, where great music wins."

The listening data explains why the flood matters. Fully AI-generated music accounts for only 1% to 3% of streams on Deezer — but up to 85% of those streams were identified as fraudulent in 2025 and demonetized, against a baseline of 8% fraud across Deezer's entire catalog. Mass synthetic uploads are not about finding an audience. They are about finding a payout.

Spotify has taken a third path, backing the DDEX industry standard for AI disclosure in credits, which lets creators declare AI involvement in vocals, instrumentation, or post-production through their distributor's metadata feed. Its AI Credits beta launched in April 2026; Spotify says the tag is informational and tracks are not downranked for disclosing.

## Why It Matters

Apple's approach is disclosure-by-declaration. Deezer's is disclosure-by-detection. The difference determines who bears the classification burden, and how much the label is worth.

Under Apple's model, the truth of the badge depends on the honesty of thousands of distributors and aggregators — the same intermediaries through which fraudulent uploads flow. A bad actor farming royalties with 500 synthetic tracks a day has every incentive not to tag them, and Apple's stated remedy is a policy requirement rather than an automated check. Apple has detection capability, per Schusser, but has chosen not to make it the arbiter.

Deezer's model puts the burden on the platform. Its detector identifies inaudible artifacts left by specific generative models, claims 99.8% accuracy, and tags at the album rather than track level to limit false positives. Deezer filed two patents on the technique in December 2024 and began licensing it commercially in January 2026 — Billboard now uses it to identify AI tracks in its charts.

Listeners have made their preference plain. In a November 2025 Ipsos survey Deezer commissioned across 9,000 people in eight countries, 97% could not distinguish fully AI-generated music from human-made music in a blind test, and 80% agreed 100% AI-generated music should be clearly labeled. A CISAC and PMP Strategy study estimates nearly 25% of music creators' revenues are at risk by 2028 — as much as 4 billion euros.

## What to Watch

Whether Apple pairs the badge with any verification — an audit, a spot-check, or its in-house detector as a backstop. A label that only honest providers apply mostly labels honest providers. Whether Apple gives listeners a filter, not just a badge. And whether the three approaches converge on Lanternier's "shared standards," or artists end up filing different disclosures for every platform — the fragmentation DDEX was meant to prevent.
