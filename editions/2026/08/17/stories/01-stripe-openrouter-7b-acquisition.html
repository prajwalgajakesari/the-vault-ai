Stripe Agrees to Buy OpenRouter for More Than $7 Billion, Five Times Its May Valuation

5 min read 993 words 4 sources
KEY TAKEAWAY

Stripe has reportedly agreed to pay more than $7 billion for OpenRouter, 5.4x the model router's $1.3 billion valuation 90 days earlier, buying not a model but the metering-and-routing layer through which roughly 100 trillion tokens of monthly inference traffic already flows.

Ninety days ago, OpenRouter was a 30-person startup that had just talked investors into a $1.3 billion valuation. On Saturday, according to Bloomberg, Stripe agreed to buy it for more than $7 billion — a 5.4x markup on a price tag that was itself considered rich in May, and one of the largest sums ever paid for a company whose core product is, essentially, a switchboard.

Bloomberg reported Sunday that "Stripe Inc. has finalized an agreement to acquire OpenRouter Inc., a startup that helps companies switch between artificial intelligence models, for more than $7 billion," citing people familiar with the matter. The outlet cautioned that the final price could still move and that the discussions have not been made public. A Stripe spokesperson told TechCrunch the company does not comment on rumors or speculation; OpenRouter declined to comment.

The deal did not come out of nowhere. The Wall Street Journal reported last month that Stripe was in talks to buy OpenRouter, with a figure closer to $10 billion floated at the time — meaning the number came down, not up, as negotiations closed. Stripe also already processes payments for OpenRouter, giving it an unusually clear view of how much money moves through the startup's pipes before it made an offer.

What Stripe is actually buying

OpenRouter, founded in 2023 by Alex Atallah and Louis Vichy, sells one API endpoint reaching more than 400 AI models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, xAI, DeepSeek and dozens of smaller providers. Developers write a single integration; OpenRouter handles routing, failover, rate limits and unified billing, deciding per request which model gets the job based on price, latency and reliability. It says it serves roughly 8 million users.

The volume explains the price. At its May Series B, OpenRouter disclosed traffic of 25 trillion tokens per week — roughly 100 trillion a month, a fivefold jump in six months. A measurable slice of global inference demand is being metered, priced and invoiced through one private company's software.

"Running inference at scale is fundamentally a multi-model problem. The era of picking a single model is over," Atallah said when the round was announced. "Success now depends on continuously routing across a changing market. Because OpenRouter sits in the flow of production traffic, we can optimize every request for cost, performance, and reliability in real time."

That clause — in the flow of production traffic — is the asset, and it is the exact structural position Stripe occupies in commerce.

An analogy that turned into a purchase order

Atallah has spent the past year describing OpenRouter as the Stripe of AI, a pitch he repeated when the $113 million Series B closed in May. Alphabet's CapitalG led that round, joined by Nvidia's NVentures, ServiceNow Ventures, MongoDB Ventures, Snowflake Ventures, Databricks Ventures, Andreessen Horowitz and Menlo Ventures. Sequoia and a16z had backed the earlier $40 million seed and Series A.

CapitalG made the same comparison in its own words. "Every platform shift creates infrastructure gaps: from Cloudflare with the internet and Stripe with digital payments, to Databricks with data and AI," partner Mo Jomaa said at the time. "These infrastructure gaps create opportunities for generational businesses to solve real customer needs. OpenRouter is solving the infrastructure gap for inference in the AI era."

Colleague Jane Alexander went further, calling OpenRouter "uniquely positioned to become the data clearinghouse and unified intelligence layer for AI models." Three months later, the original Stripe priced that analogy at $7 billion.

For Atallah, it is a sharp second act: he co-founded OpenSea, the NFT marketplace that raised more than $400 million before its volumes collapsed, stepped down in July 2022, and started OpenRouter less than a year later.

Why It Matters

Stripe is not buying a model, a research lab or a chip supply. It is buying a tollbooth — and, more valuably, the traffic logs from that tollbooth.

Payment networks have long known that the most durable business in commerce is not any single merchant but the ledger that sees all of them. Visa and Mastercard sell transaction data to hedge funds and economists because it predicts the economy weeks before earnings and months before government statistics. OpenRouter is the closest thing AI has to that ledger: real-time visibility into which models win which workloads, how fast traffic migrates when a provider cuts prices, and where enterprise inference spend is actually landing versus where press releases claim it is.

It also hedges Stripe against the question every payments company now faces: what happens when agents, not humans, initiate transactions. Model access, usage metering and consumption billing are converging into one problem, and owning the routing layer puts Stripe upstream of it.

The risk is the reason the asset is valuable at all. OpenRouter works because developers believe the routing is neutral, and they can leave for direct integrations the instant it looks commercially motivated. Writing in Forbes, EQUS.ai chief executive Sandy Carter framed the tension bluntly: "Stripe would be paying a premium for trust, and trust is the one thing that could be broken." Every obvious way to monetize the data — steering traffic to partners, mining it for training — is also a way to destroy what was purchased.

What to Watch

Neither company has confirmed the deal, so watch first for an official announcement and whether the final number holds above $7 billion or slides again, as it already did from the WSJ's reported $10 billion. After that: whether OpenRouter keeps operating as an independent, provider-agnostic gateway or gets folded into Stripe's billing stack; whether OpenAI, Anthropic and Google renegotiate commercial terms now that their aggregator answers to a payments giant; whether OpenRouter's widely cited public model rankings survive new ownership; and whether antitrust reviewers take an interest in a payments processor owning the metering layer for AI compute. Then there is the read-through for rivals — a $7 billion comp just repriced every AI gateway startup in the market.
