OpenAI has told Elon Musk it is taking its models back. On August 28, 2026, the company notified SpaceX that it intends to wind down the contract supplying OpenAI models to Cursor, the AI coding tool SpaceX absorbed earlier this month in a $60 billion all-stock deal, with a proposed shutoff date of November 12, 2026. The reason OpenAI gave was not price, capacity, or strategy. It was trust.

The trigger was a change-of-control clause. OpenAI said its custom agreement with Anysphere, Cursor's parent company, gave it a limited window to cancel after an ownership change, and SpaceX's acquisition — announced June 16 and closed August 14 — opened it. OpenAI is not slamming the door immediately. **"To maximize the time that developers can retain access to our models through Cursor, we are giving the maximum notice provided by our contract,"** the company wrote in a post published under the OpenAI byline. **"This decision was incredibly tough, as we care deeply about our models being broadly available for developers. We are making this choice because we cannot be confident that SpaceX will use our technology within our terms of service, based on our experience with Elon Musk's companies violating contracts."**

That last clause is the whole story. OpenAI laid out two specific grievances, both now folded into the same corporate parent. After Musk acquired Twitter — since merged into SpaceX — the company broke the terms of a licensing deal that had given OpenAI access to the full tweet firehose for roughly $2 million a year to train ChatGPT; Musk cut off that access in December 2022 after deciding the price was too low. Then, under oath earlier this year, Musk admitted that xAI, also now part of SpaceX, had distilled OpenAI model outputs to train Grok, a violation of OpenAI's terms of service that closely mirror xAI's own. OpenAI framed the November cutoff as the predictable consequence of dealing with a counterparty that has a documented pattern.

A second justification points forward rather than back. OpenAI cited Astra, its forthcoming model that the company has already flagged as potentially reaching the highest cybersecurity risk tier it tracks. As capabilities advance, OpenAI wrote, it faces a new level of accountability to ensure partners comply with its terms. The practical upshot: OpenAI will hold the cancellation to the latest date permitted while declining to ship any future models to Cursor at all. Existing models keep flowing until November 12. Astra never arrives.

Cursor's response was fast and deliberately deflationary. Co-founder and CEO Michael Truell posted within a day that OpenAI models account for only about five percent of Cursor's AI traffic, and that his team is in discussions with OpenAI to find a resolution. **"Cursor was one of the very first users of OpenAI, we've worked closely with their team for years, and we've trusted their platform to be neutral infrastructure for our business,"** Truell wrote. That phrase — neutral infrastructure — is the sharpest thing anyone said this week. It is a claim about what a model API is supposed to be, and OpenAI has just answered it.

Thibault Sottiaux, who works on OpenAI's developer platform, tried to draw the boundary tightly. **"It boils down to trust,"** he wrote, stressing that individual developers can still bring their own OpenAI API keys inside Cursor and that OpenAI will keep supporting its Codex IDE extension for Cursor users. The workaround is real but partial: personal API keys cover local Chat and Agent requests, and do not cover Cursor Tab, Auto, Cloud Agents, Background Agents, Automations, the CLI, or the API and SDK surfaces that heavier engineering teams depend on.

Anthropic moved within hours to occupy the gap. Tom Brown, Anthropic's co-founder and chief compute officer, posted that Cursor has been a trusted partner since Claude Sonnet 3.5 and that Anthropic will keep expanding compute capacity for Claude models inside Cursor — a pointed offer of stability aimed squarely at the developers behind Cursor's roughly $4 billion in annualized revenue.

## Why It Matters

Strip away the Musk feud and what remains is the AI industry's most consequential unresolved question: is a frontier model an input you can build a business on, or a privilege your supplier can revoke when your cap table changes? Cursor is not a marginal customer. It hit $4 billion in annualized revenue before the acquisition, making SpaceX's $60 billion price roughly 15 times forward ARR and the largest acquisition of a venture-backed startup on record. If a company at that scale can lose access to a core supplier because of who bought it, every application-layer startup with a single-provider dependency has to reprice its own risk.

The precedents are not one-sided, which is what makes Anthropic's loyalty posturing awkward. Anthropic cut Windsurf off from Claude in June 2025 after reports that OpenAI wanted to buy it, then revoked OpenAI's own Claude API access in August 2025 over unauthorized benchmarking. The pattern is now unmistakable: model providers treat acquisition events as enforcement moments, and multi-model architecture has stopped being an engineering preference and become a survival requirement. Cursor's five percent figure suggests it internalized that lesson years ago.

## What to Watch

The first thing to watch is whether November 12 actually happens. OpenAI called the date proposed, Truell says talks are underway, and there is a plausible version of this where a renegotiated agreement with tighter compliance terms lands before the deadline. Second, watch Cursor's traffic mix — if the five percent share holds steady through the fall, SpaceX will have proved that model supply is fungible; if usage or churn moves against it, OpenAI will have proved the opposite. Third, watch for regulatory interest, because a dominant model provider cutting off a rival's newly acquired subsidiary is precisely the fact pattern antitrust authorities have been circling. And watch Astra. OpenAI has now established that access to its most capable models is conditional on who owns you. Cursor is the first test case, not the last.
