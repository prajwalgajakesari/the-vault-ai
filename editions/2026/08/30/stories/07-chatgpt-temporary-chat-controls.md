For two years ChatGPT's Temporary Chat behaved like a burner phone: nothing came in, nothing went out, nothing stuck. No memories, no custom instructions, no plugins, no trace. That made it genuinely private and, for a great many people, close to useless. On August 27, 2026, OpenAI split the difference. Temporary chats can now be personalized at the moment you open one, drawing on saved memories, custom instructions and connected plugins, and any temporary conversation can be promoted into permanent chat history with a single save.

**"We heard your feedback that sometimes a Temporary chat becomes something worth saving,"** the official ChatGPT account wrote in announcing the change. **"Now you can choose to personalize a Temporary chat with your existing memories, custom instructions, and plugins, and save it to your history if you want to keep it."**

The mechanics are narrower than the headline suggests, and the narrowness is the point. Temporary chats still open non-personalized by default. Personalization is a one-time decision made before the first message is sent, and OpenAI's updated Temporary Chat FAQ is explicit that it cannot be reversed once the conversation begins: **"You choose whether the chat is personalized when you start it. This setting cannot be changed after the conversation begins."** The information flow is deliberately one-directional. A personalized temporary chat can read your existing memories but will not create or update any while it remains temporary, and it stays out of chat history unless you save it. Plugins, which OpenAI now uses as the umbrella term for connected tools such as Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Drive, are available only in the personalized mode.

Saving is where the accounting changes. Converting a temporary chat into a regular one hands it over to your account-level settings wholesale: it enters chat history, becomes eligible to be referenced by future responses if memory is enabled, and falls under whatever model-improvement preference your account carries. A chat that was categorically excluded from training becomes a chat governed by a toggle most users have never looked at. OpenAI is upfront that the exclusion only holds while the conversation stays temporary.

## The Fine Print

Three limits deserve more attention than they will get. First, unsaved temporary chats are not instantly gone. OpenAI retains a copy for up to 30 days for safety purposes, the same window that applies to deleted regular chats under its standard retention policy. Second, personalization choice does not override the account. Account-level and workspace restrictions take priority over an individual chat's setting, meaning an enterprise admin who has disabled plugins or memory disables them inside temporary chats too. Third, and least advertised, turning personalization off does not turn off everything: the FAQ notes ChatGPT may still draw on prior conversations for limited safety and security purposes.

For business customers the picture is starker. Temporary chats remain accessible through the Enterprise Compliance API for 30 days regardless of any custom retention period a workspace has configured. Temporary is a product surface, not a cryptographic guarantee.

## Why It Matters

Memory is the strongest retention mechanic any chatbot has. A model that knows your job title, your kids' names, your code style and your dietary restrictions is measurably harder to abandon than one that does not, and switching assistants means rebuilding a profile you never explicitly wrote. Stripping personalization out of temporary chats was, in that light, a self-inflicted wound: it forced users to choose between privacy and product quality on every sensitive question. This update dissolves that tradeoff for reading while preserving it for writing, which is close to the best available answer.

It also expands the surface area of a liability OpenAI has spent eighteen months fighting in court. On January 5, 2026, District Judge Sidney H. Stein affirmed magistrate rulings requiring OpenAI to produce 20 million de-identified ChatGPT logs in the New York Times copyright litigation, over the company's objection that 99.99% of the transcripts were irrelevant. An earlier indefinite-preservation obligation ended on September 26, 2025, but the company still holds a segregated set of April to September 2025 user data under legal hold.

The company's public posture on retention has been consistently adversarial. **"This fundamentally conflicts with the privacy commitments we have made to our users,"** OpenAI COO Brad Lightcap wrote of the plaintiffs' demand. **"It abandons long-standing privacy norms and weakens privacy protections."** Sam Altman has pushed further, arguing for a legal category that does not yet exist. **"I think that's very screwed up,"** he said of the current gap, adding that **"we should have like the same concept of privacy for your conversations with AI that we do with a therapist or whatever."**

Every saved temporary chat is one more record inside the perimeter that argument is trying to protect. And research suggests users are poor judges of what that perimeter holds. A CHI 2026 study of 20 ChatGPT users by Cheng Chen, Maria D. Molina, Mengqi Liao and Eugene Cho Snyder found that most participants experienced negative expectancy violations after seeing what ChatGPT had actually remembered about them, and called for far greater visibility and control over memory design. Asking those users to make an irreversible personalization decision before they have typed a word is asking a lot.

## What to Watch

Three things. Whether OpenAI eventually flips the default from non-personalized to personalized, which would quietly reverse the privacy posture of the entire feature. Whether European regulators treat a save button that changes a conversation's training eligibility as requiring fresh consent rather than an inherited account setting, particularly in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK, which OpenAI already carves out of its retention arrangements. And whether the New York Times production ends up sweeping in conversations users believed were ephemeral. The company has built a good privacy control. Whether it can keep the promise attached to it is being decided in a Manhattan courtroom, not a product spec.
