MiniMax spent the summer doing what Chinese AI labs now treat as routine: ship a frontier-class model behind an API, then hand out the weights. On August 24, 2026, the Shanghai company closed the loop, making MiniMax M3 broadly available alongside the smaller M2.7 and putting both free on GMI Cloud for a fourteen-day window. M3 is a 428-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with a one-million-token context window and native image and video input. In almost any other month it would have been the release of the week. Instead it was the fourth of five open-weight launches inside nine days.

The architecture is the part worth reading closely. M3 activates roughly 22 billion of its 428 billion parameters per token, routing through 128 experts with four selected at each step, and carries a separate 600-million-parameter visual encoder. Critically, MiniMax trained text, image and video together from step zero across roughly 100 trillion interleaved tokens rather than grafting a vision encoder onto a finished text model — the distinction that separates natively multimodal from the retrofits that dominated 2025.

What makes a million-token window economically tractable is MiniMax Sparse Attention (MSA), which replaces quadratic attention with a pre-filtering stage that identifies relevant context blocks and attends only to those. The downstream numbers are the headline: at full 1M context, M3 uses one-twentieth the per-token compute of the previous M2 generation, with prefill 9x faster and decoding 15x faster. On NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra, throughput runs up to 10x higher than on prior-generation Hopper silicon.

The pitch is consolidation. **"As enterprise AI adoption scales, developers are increasingly forced to stitch together fragmented pipelines — separate models for text, vision, and code — leading to added complexity, higher costs, and slower iteration,"** wrote Anu Srivastava, senior technical marketing manager at NVIDIA, in the company's M3 deployment guide. M3 replaces that, she argued, with a single system spanning long-context reasoning, agentic workflows and creative tasks.

## The Numbers

Independent measurement puts M3 at the top of the open-weight pile without putting it at the top of the field. Artificial Analysis scores M3 at 55 on its Intelligence Index, five points above the text-only M2.7 and just ahead of open-weight peers Kimi K2.6 and MiMo-V2.5-Pro, both at 54. **"Once the weights are released, it will be the leading open weights model,"** the firm wrote in its launch analysis — a formulation that was, at the time, still conditional.

Component scores show where the gains landed: GPQA Diamond climbed from 87% to 93%, Humanity's Last Exam from 28% to 37%, IFBench from 76% to 83%. On MMMU-Pro, M3 scores roughly 80%, level with GPT-5.5 at xhigh effort. On GDPval-AA, which measures real-world tasks across 44 occupations and nine industries, M3 lands near 1670 — level with Claude Sonnet 4.6 (1676), behind GPT-5.5 (1769) and Claude Opus 4.8 (1890). Fireworks reports 59.0% on SWE-Bench Pro and 74.2% on MCP Atlas, and says M3 has run autonomously for 12 to 24 hours on tasks including paper reproduction and CUDA kernel optimization.

One caveat the benchmark tables bury: on AA-Omniscience, M3 attempts only 30.9% of questions, the lowest rate among current peers. That abstention buys a low hallucination rate (16.1%) at the cost of low accuracy (15.0%) — a model tuned to decline rather than guess.

Pricing runs $0.30 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output up to 512K of context, doubling to $0.60 and $2.40 between 512K and 1M.

## Why It Matters

M3's problem is that it is competing against a calendar, not a model. Z.ai's GLM-5.3-Flash landed on August 26 at 320B total and 18B active parameters, natively multimodal, 1M context, under an MIT license, at $0.15 and $0.50 per million tokens — half M3's rate on a permissive license. Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Flash arrived the same day at $0.16 and $0.47. Tencent's Hy4 Preview (770B total, 49B active) shipped two days later, with Cline reporting it leading on SWE-bench Pro. DeepSeek's V4-Flash-Vision-Exp preceded them all on August 21. Requesty's social listening corpus recorded model-launch chatter jumping from 506 mentions to 978 week over week.

Nobody in that list claimed a decisive capability lead. What moved was the price attached to the capability, and the closed labs answered in the same currency — OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Sol API pricing by more than 20% for three months. Million-token context, native multimodality and open weights are now default features of the cheap tier, not premium differentiators. The capability premium has not vanished at the very top of GDPval-AA, but the gap between an open 428B MoE at $0.30 per million and a closed frontier model at ten times that is no longer one most workloads can feel.

The trap is assuming cheap per token means cheap per task. **"The model is now the cheap part, and the expensive part is everything around it: effort settings, cache hit rate, provider choice and retry behaviour,"** wrote Thibault Jaigu, whose analysis for the AI gateway Requesty documented developers burning credits on open-weight models faster than on the closed models they replaced. One measurement found an 11x price spread between the cheapest and most expensive provider serving identical weights.

## What to Watch

The license is the first thing to check. MiniMax released M2.7's weights under a commercially restricted license, and Artificial Analysis flagged that precedent as reason to expect the same for M3. Against MIT-licensed GLM-5.3, that is a procurement disadvantage no benchmark fixes. Second, watch whether the 1M tier gets used in practice — MiniMax charges double above 512K, pricing long context as a specialty rather than a default. Third, watch MiniMax's commercial posture: the company raised $2 billion in July, and founder Yan Junjie has pledged to forgo salary until AGI. **"If we had to start over, we would have gone open source on day one,"** Yan told 36Kr in an earlier interview. The M3 rollout is that conviction tested against a market where four other labs made the same bet in the same week.
