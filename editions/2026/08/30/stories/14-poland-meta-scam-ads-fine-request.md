Poland's cybersecurity agency ran a test on Meta. CERT Polska flagged 122 advertisements it had classified as fraudulent to Facebook and Instagram. Meta refused to remove 106 of them. Ten came down; six drew no response at all. That is a rejection rate of 86.8 percent, and it is now Exhibit A in a formal request from Warsaw asking the European Commission to fine Meta 250 million euros under the Digital Services Act.

The letter went out on August 26 from Krzysztof Gawkowski, Poland's deputy prime minister and digital affairs minister, addressed to Henna Virkkunen, the Commission's executive vice-president for technological sovereignty, security and democracy. Gawkowski announced it publicly the following morning. **Inaction and ineffectiveness in combating harmful advertisements and fraudulent content targeting Poles will not be tolerated**, he wrote. **I expect the European Commission to conduct the proceedings swiftly and impose the fine. There is more than enough evidence.** He added a direct demand to the company: introduce effective tools against scams and fake ads, because **this Wild West on the platform must come to an end.**

Two days later, on August 28, Meta announced that Poland would be among the first countries in Europe to receive an upgraded AI system for detecting fraud and impersonation.

## The legal machinery

Poland cannot fine Meta. Under the DSA, enforcement against very large online platforms sits exclusively with the Commission, so the 250 million euro figure — roughly 1.1 billion zloty — is a request, not a penalty. The referral alleges breaches anchored in Articles 34 and 35, which require VLOPs to identify systemic risks flowing from the design and use of their service and apply reasonable, proportionate and effective mitigation; advertising transparency and the Article 16 notice-and-action mechanism are also in scope. The DSA ceiling is 6 percent of global turnover — several billion euros for Meta. Warsaw's ask is deliberately smaller.

Commission spokesperson Thomas Regnier confirmed receipt and said the material would feed existing work. **We will certainly examine all this evidence and take it into account in our investigation**, he said, adding that **fraudulent advertisements and deepfakes of this kind are, of course, a very serious problem and we take it seriously.** Meta is already under multiple DSA proceedings, including preliminary findings over deficient notice-and-action mechanisms and dark patterns, and a probe into minors' access opened in April 2026.

Regnier also aimed a return volley at Warsaw. Poland remains the one EU member state without a fully empowered national Digital Services Coordinator, after President Karol Nawrocki vetoed the implementing law in January, calling it a vehicle for **administrative censorship**. **To properly enforce the DSA, we also rely on member states**, Regnier said, **and in this case we have a serious problem with Poland.** A country demanding faster Brussels enforcement has not built its own enforcement office.

## What Meta is shipping

Meta's Polish-language announcement frames the timing as defensive. The company said it was seeing **a campaign that distorts the picture of Meta's actions and motivations in this area**, and that scams **harm individual victims while undermining trust in the advertising ecosystem on which our business model rests** — translated from the Polish.

The substance has two parts. First, an upgraded AI impersonation detector. Meta says its existing systems already cut user reports concerning the most frequently misused public-figure likenesses by more than 80 percent, and the new model targets criminals who learned to route around the old one. Poland is in the first European deployment group. Second, mandatory identity verification for 100 percent of financial-services advertisers targeting Polish audiences, within weeks. That ladders into a global target: 90 percent of Meta's worldwide ad revenue from verified advertisers by end-2026, up from 70 percent in 2025.

Meta's own numbers for Poland: 137,000 fraudulent ads removed between July 2025 and June 2026, more than 88 percent before any user reported them, plus 380,000 pieces of content. Scam ad report rates in Poland fell 83 percent between July 2024 and June 2026.

Gawkowski was unimpressed. **Meta is bending and making further declarations**, he posted. **Trust in Meta's actions does not exist, and now only concrete results can restore it.**

## Why it matters

The interesting move is not the fine request. It is the sequence: a minister publishes a controlled experiment, a rejection rate and a euro figure, and within 48 hours a platform ships a technical roadmap with a percentage target and a deadline. That is enforcement pressure converting into product specification without a non-compliance decision, without a court, without anyone paying anything. The DSA's systemic-risk articles are vague enough that mitigation is negotiated rather than prescribed, and Regnier has said the Commission's preferred outcome is commitments, not penalties.

The second thing worth noticing is that AI sits on both sides of the ledger. The scams Poland is complaining about are largely deepfaked investment pitches using the faces of InPost founder Rafal Brzoska, President Nawrocki and other public figures — generation costs near zero, volume effectively unbounded. Meta's answer is a better classifier. The regulatory question the DSA never quite asks is what detection rate counts as reasonable and effective when the attacker's marginal cost is a prompt.

The stakes are not abstract. CERT Polska logged 260,800 confirmed incidents in 2025, up 152 percent year on year, roughly 98 percent of them internet fraud, with minimum documented losses of 537 million zloty. Nearly 99,000 fraudulent investment domains hit the national warning list, more than double the prior year. Europol now calls fraud the fastest-growing area of internet-enabled organised crime.

## What to watch

Whether the Commission folds Poland's evidence into a formal proceeding or lets it sit as supporting material. Whether Meta's financial-advertiser verification lands on schedule, and whether CERT Polska re-runs its test — the same 122-ad methodology applied in November would be the most useful data point anyone could produce. And whether Warsaw seats its Digital Services Coordinator, because until it does, every Polish complaint travels to Brussels and waits in line behind everyone else's.
