Google Consolidates AI Power in California as Hassabis Steps Up to Chairman

Mountain View, Calif. — Google on Wednesday reshaped the command structure of its most valuable asset, elevating a little-known engineer to run the day-to-day of its AI empire and moving Demis Hassabis, the Nobel laureate who built Google DeepMind into a global force, into a chairman's seat focused on the endgame he has chased his entire career: artificial general intelligence.

In a joint memo to staff titled "The next chapter of our AI momentum," Alphabet chief executive Sundar Pichai announced that Koray Kavukcuoglu, DeepMind's longtime chief technology officer, will become senior vice president of Google DeepMind and report directly to Pichai. Kavukcuoglu, who also holds the newly created title of Chief AI Architect, will oversee Gemini model development, frontier research, and the Gemini app and developer teams. As part of the shift, he is relocating from London to California, tightening the center of gravity of Google's AI operation around its Mountain View headquarters.

Hassabis, who won a share of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for AlphaFold, is stepping back from operational management to become Chair of Google DeepMind and Chief Scientist of Alphabet, while continuing to lead the drug-discovery spinout Isomorphic Labs.

A leader who feels AGI 'close at hand'

Hassabis framed the move as a deliberate pivot toward the technology's decisive phase. "We have arrived at a pivotal moment in human history," he wrote to employees. "I've been working towards AGI my whole life and now, like many of you, I feel it is close at hand. It's critical that we collectively get the next steps right to ensure this all goes well for humanity."

He said the new role would give him "the time and space to focus on the big picture," adding that he would "lean into" Isomorphic Labs, where he says progress has been "extremely rapid and promising." Hassabis has long argued that curing disease is AI's highest calling. "It's time for AI to prove its unequivocal value to the world," he wrote, "and what better way to demonstrate that than to help finally cure diseases like cancer." Notably, Hassabis will keep his base at DeepMind's new London "Platform 37" offices, even as the operational leadership migrates west.

Pichai praised Kavukcuoglu, a 13-year DeepMind veteran who started the lab's deep learning team and led breakthroughs such as WaveNet and the DQN reinforcement-learning system. "I look forward to seeing him lead GDM into this next chapter," Pichai wrote, describing Hassabis's work on AGI as "vitally important to Alphabet and humanity."

The reshuffle came bundled with a bigger surprise: Jeff Dean, the Google Senior Fellow whose infrastructure work underpins much of the modern company, is leaving after 27 years. Dean and fellow Senior Fellow Sanjay Ghemawat are launching an independent public benefit corporation to accelerate discoveries in machine learning, science and engineering, with Google as a founding investor and cloud partner.

Circling the wagons in Mountain View

The geographic message is as important as the org chart. By pulling Kavukcuoglu to California and moving other senior figures, including AI coding lead Sebastian Borgeaud, from the U.K. to the Bay Area, Google is physically consolidating decision-making at a moment when its rivals are only a short drive away. OpenAI and Anthropic are both headquartered in San Francisco, and proximity has become a competitive weapon in an industry where the scarcest resource is not compute but people.

That talent war has not been running in Google's favor. DeepMind has bled marquee names: Noam Shazeer, a co-author of the foundational "Attention Is All You Need" transformer paper, decamped for OpenAI, while AlphaFold co-creator John Jumper — who shared that 2024 Nobel with Hassabis — left for Anthropic. Industry data circulated over the past year suggested engineers were leaving DeepMind for Anthropic at a roughly 11-to-1 clip, an imbalance that unnerved leadership. Concentrating authority under a single operational chief in California is, in part, an answer to that churn: fewer reporting lines, faster decisions, and a clearer story to tell recruits weighing offers from smaller, faster-moving labs.

The stakes are visible in the numbers Pichai cited. The Gemini app has crossed 950 million monthly users, Gemma open models have surpassed 900 million downloads, and Gemini 4 is on the way. Google's argument to talent and investors alike is scale: it is, in Hassabis's words, "the only company that has the full stack," from chips and data centers to cloud to frontier models to consumer apps. OpenAI and Anthropic each own only slices of that stack and depend on outside infrastructure partners.

What to watch

The open question is whether consolidation buys speed or simply centralizes risk. Kavukcuoglu is a respected researcher but an unproven manager of an operation this sprawling, and he inherits it during a punishing release cadence against OpenAI and Anthropic. Hassabis's semi-departure removes a singular scientific figurehead from daily model decisions even as he insists AGI is near.

Watch three things: whether Gemini 4 lands on time and closes any gap with rivals; whether Google's talent outflow slows now that a clearer California command structure is in place; and whether Isomorphic Labs, freshly reinforced by its founder, delivers the clinical milestones that would validate Hassabis's bet that AI's greatest prize lies in medicine, not chat.
