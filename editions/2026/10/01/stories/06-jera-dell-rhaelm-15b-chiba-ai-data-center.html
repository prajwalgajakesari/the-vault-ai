JERA, Dell and RHAELM Plan $15 Billion, 400MW AI Data Center Inside a Japanese Power Plant

5 min read 934 words 4 sources
KEY TAKEAWAY

By putting a 400MW AI campus behind the meter at its Chiba power station, JERA, Dell and RHAELM aim to skip Japan's grid queues and build a model they can repeat to reach multi-gigawatt AI capacity in the 2030s.

Japan's biggest power company has decided the fastest way to get electricity to an AI data center is to stop sending it anywhere else. JERA, which generates roughly a third of the country's electricity, signed a memorandum of understanding on October 1 with Dell Technologies and AI infrastructure developer RHAELM to build an AI campus of up to 400MW right next to its Chiba Thermal Power Station. Total capital deployment is expected to top $15 billion, or about 2.3 trillion yen.

At that size, Chiba would be the largest single-site AI infrastructure deployment in Japan and one of the largest in Asia, according to the companies. Operations are targeted to begin around 2028.

The deal

Each partner covers one layer of the stack. JERA brings the land and the power: the campus will draw electricity "behind the meter" from JERA's operating generation assets at Chiba instead of waiting in line for a conventional grid connection. RHAELM, a UK-based developer that bills itself as a sovereign AI infrastructure builder, will lead project delivery and co-develop the site with JERA. Dell supplies the compute through its Dell AI Factory portfolio, which is pre-integrated, rack-scale AI hardware built to be deployed quickly.

The money comes from Wall Street. Apollo Global Management intends to act as RHAELM's strategic investment and financing partner on the Chiba project, giving the developer a deep-pocketed backer for a buildout that will mostly be spent on land, power infrastructure, construction and AI equipment.

JERA Global CEO and Chair Yukio Kani pitched the utility as the natural anchor. "JERA is uniquely positioned to power Japan's AI ambitions," he said, noting that the company already supplies "nearly all of the power consumed in the Tokyo metropolitan area," with operations covering the full LNG value chain from fuel procurement to generation.

A template, not a one-off

The companies are clear that Chiba is meant to be the first of several sites. The MoU sets out a "standardised, repeatable and scalable model" in which power generation, electrical infrastructure, cooling and compute are designed together from the start, not worked out one project at a time. JERA and RHAELM plan to test the approach at other JERA power stations, aiming for multi-gigawatt AI infrastructure capacity across Japan in the 2030s. They also intend to look at markets outside Japan over time.

The Financial Times reported that the wider multi-site program could eventually reach $140 billion in investment, according to Channel Insider. JERA's official announcement does not put a figure on the expansion.

Dell's Arthur Lewis, President of the company's Infrastructure Solutions Group, presented the deal as a sovereignty play as much as a capacity play. "Deploying AI infrastructure at national scale requires a foundation built for control, flexibility and growth from day one," Lewis said. He added that the collaboration is designed to create "the compute capacity Japan needs to develop and scale its own AI innovation."

RHAELM CEO and Chief Revenue Officer Bradd Lewis put the speed argument most directly. "RHAELM was built to solve exactly this kind of challenge taking stranded power capacity and turning it into sovereign AI infrastructure," he said. Co-locating at Chiba with Dell as the compute standard, he argued, means the partners "can deliver a 400MW facility years ahead of a conventional grid-connected timeline."

Why It Matters

Power is now the main constraint on AI capacity, ahead of chips, and Chiba is a direct response to that. Grid interconnection queues in the US, Europe and Asia now run for years, and hyperscalers are increasingly building next to generation itself: nuclear plants in Pennsylvania, gas fields in Texas, and now an LNG-fired thermal station on Tokyo Bay. Behind-the-meter supply skips the queue. The trade-off is that the data center depends on one plant and, in this case, on imported natural gas.

The deal also fits Japanese industrial policy. Tokyo's "Watt-Bit Collaboration" initiative, run by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry and the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications, aims to plan electricity, telecom and data-center buildout together, and its GX Strategic Area policy encourages data-center clusters near power sources. A utility that controls a third of national generation and has sites to spare is a strong partner for that kind of plan.

For Dell, the deal is a showcase win for the AI Factory brand outside the US, at a scale that puts it in the same conversation as the multi-hundred-megawatt campuses being built for frontier model labs. For Apollo, it is another sign that private credit is becoming a major source of financing for AI infrastructure.

Four hundred megawatts of AI compute on one site would also change Japan's position. Domestic AI capacity has lagged far behind the US, and "sovereign AI" (compute that stays on Japanese soil, under Japanese control) has become a recurring theme in Tokyo's tech policy. Chiba would be the largest step yet in that direction.

What to Watch

This is still a memorandum of understanding, not a final investment decision, so the next milestones are binding agreements: a definitive power-supply contract between JERA and RHAELM, Apollo's financing commitment, and above all the name of the anchor tenant or tenants who will actually rent 400MW of GPUs. Watch which accelerators Dell puts in the racks, whether a second JERA site is named before Chiba comes online, and how regulators and the public react to tying a major new electricity load to gas-fired generation while Japan is still trying to meet its decarbonization targets. If the 2028 date holds, Chiba could become the template for how countries without spare grid capacity build AI infrastructure.
