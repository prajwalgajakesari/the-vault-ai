Micron Posts Record $54.2 Billion Quarter as AI Memory Demand Rewrites the Chip Market

5 min read 999 words 4 sources
KEY TAKEAWAY

Micron's $54.23B quarter at an 87% gross margin, more than 75% of 2027 output already committed, and $61.5B in guidance suggest AI has turned memory from a boom-and-bust commodity into a strategic bottleneck.

Micron Technology just booked more revenue in a single quarter than it did in the whole of fiscal 2025, and it says the next quarter will be bigger. The Boise-based memory maker reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $54.23 billion on September 30, up from $41.46 billion the prior quarter and $11.32 billion a year earlier. That is roughly a 379% increase year over year and the company's sixth straight quarterly revenue record. The cause is the same one behind most chip-industry headlines this year: AI systems need enormous amounts of memory, and there isn't enough to go around.

The Numbers

GAAP net income for the quarter, which ended September 3, came to $37.70 billion, or $32.87 per diluted share. On a non-GAAP basis Micron earned $33.42 per share, ahead of the roughly $31.61 analysts expected, according to CNBC. Non-GAAP gross margin reached 87.0%, up from 45.7% a year ago. Non-GAAP operating margin was 82.3%, a number usually associated with software licensing rather than a company that runs fabs.

Cash generation kept pace. Operating cash flow was $43.97 billion in the quarter, compared with $5.73 billion a year earlier, and adjusted free cash flow was $33.20 billion. Micron ended the fiscal year with $73.48 billion in cash, marketable investments and restricted cash.

For the full fiscal year, revenue was $133.19 billion against $37.38 billion in fiscal 2025. GAAP net income came to $84.97 billion. Net capital expenditures for the year totaled $27.37 billion, and the company said it will keep increasing investment in technology and manufacturing capacity.

Every business unit grew sharply. Core Data Center revenue was $18.0 billion, up from $1.58 billion a year ago, which CNBC described as an 11-fold jump. Cloud Memory brought in $16.28 billion, Mobile and Client $13.11 billion, and Automotive and Embedded $6.82 billion. Gross margins in all four units were between 83% and 90%.

Guidance and the Executive Pitch

Micron guided fiscal first-quarter 2027 revenue to $61.5 billion, plus or minus $1.5 billion, with non-GAAP gross margin of about 86.25% and non-GAAP EPS of $38.15, plus or minus $1.00. Analysts polled by LSEG had expected about $57 billion in revenue and $35.40 in adjusted EPS, according to CNBC.

"Micron delivered record fiscal 2026 results, and we expect an even stronger fiscal 2027," Chairman and CEO Sanjay Mehrotra said in the earnings release. "AI is becoming Super Intelligence (SI), and memory enhances this intelligence and the competitiveness of our customers' platforms."

On the earnings call, Mehrotra said much of next year's supply is already sold. "Regarding my comment on 2027, more than 75% of our output is already committed," he told analysts, adding that this covers customers under Micron's new Strategic Customer Agreements as well as others. He also said high-bandwidth memory (HBM) "continues to grow faster than conventional DRAM through 2028." Management said calendar 2027 HBM prices are well above 2026 levels and that most of next year's HBM volume has already been sold at those prices.

Chief Financial Officer Mark Murphy focused on the balance sheet. "Our balance sheet has never been stronger, and we expect it to strengthen further even as we increase investment in technology and needed capacity," he said. Murphy also said Micron intends to seek a larger buyback authorization and return more capital starting in December.

On the product side, Micron said it has begun sampling 1-gamma-based LPDDR6 memory to multiple "physical AI" markets, meaning robotics, vehicles and other machines that run AI at the edge. It also began sampling what it calls the industry's first 512GB DDR5 RDIMM server modules. Revenue from its server LPDDR SOCAMM portfolio more than doubled from the previous quarter, and its PCIe Gen 5 and Gen 6 SSDs are now shipping to large customers for KV-cache workloads, which hold the context data that large language models need during inference.

Why It Matters

For decades, memory was the classic commodity part of the semiconductor business. It went through booms and busts, prices collapsed whenever supply caught up, and margins rarely stayed high for long. Micron's numbers suggest AI has changed that, at least for now. GPUs and custom accelerators need large stacks of HBM to stay fed, and inference at scale needs high-capacity DRAM and fast SSDs. As a result, memory has become one of the tightest bottlenecks in the AI buildout.

The Strategic Customer Agreements may be the most important change. On the call, Mehrotra called multi-year commitments "a fundamental change to the nature of this industry," because they give Micron long lead times to plan investment and supply. The balance sheet shows how this works in practice: noncurrent customer contract liabilities rose from $568 million to $12.9 billion in one quarter, reflecting $12.75 billion in customer deposits for the year. Hyperscalers and AI labs are paying upfront to secure future supply, something that was rare in the memory market before now.

The downstream effects reach well beyond Micron. Higher memory costs push up the price of every AI server, which feeds into cloud pricing and, eventually, into what businesses pay for AI services. When supply is committed through 2027, a company that didn't sign contracts early can't simply buy more.

What to Watch

Investors were less excited than the results might suggest. CNBC reported that the stock barely moved after the report, after rising more than 500% over the past year, and some commentators have warned that an 87% gross margin leaves little room for upside in a cyclical industry. Over the next several quarters, watch whether Micron hits its $61.5 billion target, how quickly new capacity comes online at Micron and at rivals SK Hynix and Samsung, and whether reports that one large customer is cutting back on HBM specifications turn into a real slowdown in demand. Mehrotra dismissed that risk on the call and said he sees 2027 and 2028 as tighter than 2026. The scale of the buyback increase planned for December will also show how confident management is that this cycle will last.
