Broadcom to Lend Anthropic Up to $42 Billion to Fund Its $125 Billion TPU Compute Commitment

5 min read 962 words 4 sources
KEY TAKEAWAY

Broadcom is now Anthropic's chip supplier, lessor and lender, and that leaves both companies' AI growth partly funded from Broadcom's own balance sheet, a conflict Anthropic flags in its own IPO filing.

Broadcom has agreed to lend Anthropic up to $42 billion so the AI lab can lease computing capacity built on chips Broadcom helps design. That makes the semiconductor giant Anthropic's supplier, its landlord and its lender all at once. The arrangement surfaced in Anthropic's IPO prospectus, which Reuters reviewed and reported on Thursday. It is the clearest sign yet that the companies selling AI compute are now also paying for their customers to buy it.

The facility is sized to cover about a third of Anthropic's $125.2 billion, five-year commitment to lease tensor processing unit (TPU) capacity. TPUs are the AI accelerators Google designs together with Broadcom. The next-generation capacity starts coming online in 2027, the same year Anthropic is expected to become Broadcom's largest compute customer.

How the Deal Is Structured

According to the filing, the financing would come through convertible notes. That means Broadcom's loan could eventually turn into an equity stake in one of the most valuable private companies in the world. Broadcom can also bring in a financing partner. Anthropic said it does not expect any notes to be sold before its IPO is completed. Reuters reported that the listing could value the company at around $2 trillion.

The prospectus shows the relationship already had collateral attached. In April 2026, Anthropic put cash into a restricted account for Broadcom's benefit, and the filing says it may have to add more in certain circumstances. That was the same month Anthropic announced an expanded agreement with Google and Broadcom for multiple gigawatts of next-generation TPU capacity starting in 2027.

The filing also contains clauses that credit investors will look at closely. Anthropic warns that some payment or performance defaults could make a large share of its lease obligations due at once. In that scenario, it could make only limited use of the $42 billion facility to cover them.

Anthropic is open about the tension in Broadcom's many roles. The prospectus says Broadcom's position as both hardware supplier and financing partner creates "potential conflicts of interest" that could affect Anthropic's access to computing capacity. It adds that Broadcom's decisions on pricing and hardware could limit how much infrastructure Anthropic can secure. Broadcom declined to comment to Reuters, and Anthropic also declined to comment.

A Deepening Loop

The $42 billion facility adds to a relationship that was already growing quickly. In June, Broadcom joined Apollo and Blackstone to launch an AI infrastructure financing platform. Its first transaction was $35 billion to support more than one gigawatt of Anthropic compute built around Broadcom XPUs and networking. In August, Bloomberg reported that Broadcom was looking to raise more than $60 billion in debt to fund chips for Anthropic.

Analysts see Broadcom following a path Nvidia has already taken. Nvidia has repeatedly used its balance sheet to support demand for its hardware, most visibly by investing in CoreWeave and then agreeing to buy unsold CoreWeave capacity.

"Nvidia is putting in place a massive amount of its balance sheet, and Broadcom is having to follow suit," Seaport Research analyst Jay Goldberg told Reuters.

Others worry about how much of the AI buildout now depends on a small number of labs. "It feels that there's quite a concentrated bet right now on two companies being able to generate enough revenues to support all the financing that's happened," Robert Leitao, managing partner of Rothschild & Co, told Reuters.

The scale of those revenue needs is large. Anthropic's prospectus discloses at least $518 billion in infrastructure commitments over the coming decade with Google, Amazon, Microsoft and Broadcom. It also shows that the company lost $42 billion in 2025 even as its revenue grew 12-fold. Broadcom, for its part, projects about $115 billion in AI semiconductor revenue in fiscal 2027 and $230 billion in fiscal 2028. Much of that growth depends on Anthropic.

Why It Matters

The deal changes how investors need to read both companies' numbers. For Broadcom, a $42 billion lending facility to a single customer makes its AI revenue targets depend on questions usually asked of banks. How concentrated is the customer base? How enforceable are the contracts? What happens if a major counterparty misses a payment? Vendor financing can pull demand forward and lock in volume, but it trades some margin for credit risk. The convertible feature adds equity upside, which makes it harder to separate Broadcom's chip business from its financing exposure.

For Anthropic, the facility solves an immediate problem. Frontier labs need hundreds of billions of dollars of guaranteed compute, and few lenders can underwrite that scale. Having the supplier fund part of it secures access to scarce capacity. The cost is a deeper dependence on one partner whose pricing and hardware decisions Anthropic itself calls a risk factor.

The deal also fits a wider pattern that regulators are watching. A Bank for International Settlements bulletin published Thursday found that 46.4% of AI-to-AI investment deal value between 2021 and 2025 involved companies with commercial supply-chain ties to each other. The BIS has warned that financial stress at one highly connected AI firm could spread through its suppliers, investors and customers. Circular financing does not prove that AI demand is artificial. But it does mean headline revenue, compute commitments and investment flows increasingly have to be read together.

What to Watch

The next test comes when Anthropic's IPO prices and public investors can weigh these obligations directly. Watch whether Broadcom names a financing partner for the notes, how much of the facility is actually drawn once TPU capacity arrives in 2027, and whether Anthropic has to top up its restricted cash account. Also watch whether Broadcom's next earnings call gives more detail on how much of its $115 billion fiscal 2027 AI target depends on a customer it is now financing.
