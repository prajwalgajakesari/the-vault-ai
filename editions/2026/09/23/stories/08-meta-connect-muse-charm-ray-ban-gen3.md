# Meta Connect Bets on Muse Hardware: A Palm-Sized Charm, Ray-Ban Gen 3 and Camera-Free Luna Glasses

Mark Zuckerberg saved the strangest reveal for last. After more than an hour of glasses, headsets and demos at Meta's Menlo Park campus on Wednesday evening, the Meta chief executive held up a small handheld gadget, told the audience it was "actually real," and gave it a name: **Muse Charm**. The palm-sized device exists for one purpose, to put Meta's new Muse AI agent in your pocket, and it anchored a Connect 2026 keynote in which nearly every product on stage was pitched as a new way to talk to the same assistant.

The Charm was the headline, but it was not the only hardware. Meta also launched the **Ray-Ban Meta Gen 3** at $449, a camera-free audio model called **Ray-Ban Meta Audio** (the product that had circulated under the Luna name) at $349, and a slim $1,299.99 **Meta VR Glasses** headset that will not arrive until spring 2027. All of it is being wired into Muse, the personal agent Meta released on September 8 and which is powered by its Muse Spark model.

## A 'holdable' built around one assistant

According to Bloomberg and CNBC, the Muse Charm came out of Meta's new design lab led by Alan Dye, the former head of interface design at Apple, working together with Meta's Superintelligence AI group. Zuckerberg framed it as a faster way to reach the agent than a phone. People will be able to speak to the gadget and interact with their agents "without having to unlock a phone or open an app," he said, according to CNBC. "We've packed a lot of technology with this little guy."

Meta calls the device a "holdable." 9to5Mac reports it has a roughly 2-inch OLED touchscreen with a customizable Muse character at the center, whose look, outfits and voice users can change. It has front- and rear-facing cameras, speakers, microphones, 5G connectivity and a USB-C port. Bloomberg reports that the cameras are there mainly to give Muse visual context for analysis and actions, not to replace a phone camera. Unite.AI's account of the keynote says a conversation starts when the user taps a fingerprint sensor in the corner and starts talking, and that Zuckerberg said the full real-time voice and avatar stack runs on the device. Two Charms can recognize and interact with each other when they are close together.

The Charm is not finished yet. Zuckerberg said Meta had built only a few units and still needs to settle the finishing materials and component layout. Meta said it plans to ship in time for the holidays, and 9to5Mac, citing Bloomberg, says sales are expected to begin in December. Meta gave no price. Bloomberg reports the Charm is expected to cost about as much as a smartwatch.

## Glasses for people who want the camera, and for people who don't

The Ray-Ban Meta Gen 3, whose sunglasses and optical versions leaked earlier under the codenames Aperol and Bellini, went on sale the day of the keynote starting at $449, in Wayfarer, Aviator and Zena styles. It keeps the 12-megapixel camera and 3K video of the previous generation. What changes is a slimmer frame, a customizable action button, a six-microphone array that Meta says removes more than 90 percent of background noise, and a battery rated for up to nine hours, one hour longer than Gen 2.

The more notable launch may be the one without a lens. Ray-Ban Meta Audio is Meta's first pair of camera-free smart glasses. It weighs 43 grams, uses open-ear speakers, and is rated for 12 hours of battery life plus another 48 hours from the case, according to The Gadgeteer. It still handles music, calls, live translation and Muse. Preorders opened at $349, with shipping on October 13. Meta also introduced a $249 Meta Adventurer, which it calls its cheapest smart glasses so far, and said it will offer more than 100 glasses options across Ray-Ban, Oakley and Meta Glasses by the end of the year.

At the high end, the Meta VR Glasses weigh about 100 grams. Crypto Briefing described this segment as a preview of the Project Phoenix mixed-reality headset. The glasses drive what Meta calls a 5K micro-OLED display and are connected by cable to a pocket puck that holds the Snapdragon processor, the battery and storage. They cost $1,299.99 and ship in spring 2027.

## Why It Matters

Meta is making a bet that the rest of Big Tech has so far approached with caution: an AI agent is worth building dedicated hardware around. Muse gives the bet some momentum. The app reached the top of the U.S. App Store and Google Play within days of its launch, according to 9to5Mac. Meta also comes into the category with a real hardware business behind it. Meta and EssilorLuxottica sold more than 7 million glasses units last year. The Charm shows Meta is not willing to depend on Apple's or Google's phones as the main way people reach its assistant.

The camera-free Audio model also recognizes a problem. Meta's camera glasses have drawn steady criticism over privacy, and removing the lens at a lower price gives cautious buyers a way to use Muse without being seen as recording. The Charm pulls the other way, since it carries two cameras, and Meta's claim that they exist mostly to give the agent context is likely to be tested. The broader risk is the familiar one for AI gadgets: a dedicated device has to be meaningfully faster or more pleasant than the phone already in someone's pocket.

## What to Watch

The open questions are price and timing. Meta has promised more Charm details soon, so the price, and whether the device actually ships in December, will show how serious the push is. Other things to track are Ray-Ban Meta Audio's first reviews after October 13, the promised Muse upgrade on existing glasses in the coming weeks, and whether a competing AI gadget, especially OpenAI's long-rumored device, hits the market before the holidays.
