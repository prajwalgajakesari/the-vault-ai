# Chinese Open Models Now Handle Most OpenRouter Traffic as Price and Agentic Coding Reshape Adoption

Seven months ago, Chinese AI models were a rounding error on the platforms developers use to pick and switch between models. Now they do most of the work. In the week of September 14, Chinese models accounted for between **57% and 67%** of all tokens processed on OpenRouter, up from 6% to 13% in February, according to usage data shared with CNBC. On Vercel, a second big developer platform, their share climbed from 11% in January to **55% in August**.

The numbers point to a quick change in where developers send their everyday workloads, and the reason is mostly price. What they don't show is a change in where the money goes. U.S. frontier models from labs like OpenAI and Anthropic still collect more total spending, even as they lose volume on these routing platforms.

## Cheap enough, good enough

The executives who run these platforms point to two things: a jump in capability this year, especially in coding and multi-step agentic work, and a large price gap. Peter Walker, head of insights at OpenRouter, told CNBC that Chinese open-source models released in 2026 can now credibly handle advanced agentic use cases, particularly coding, in a way they could not in late 2025. He also said they cost far less than most models from American labs. New releases from DeepSeek, Z.ai, Alibaba and Moonshot AI have narrowed the gap on coding tasks, even though top U.S. models still lead on most benchmarks.

Harpreet Arora, head of agentic infrastructure at Vercel, described the buying decision in plain terms. "Chinese models are becoming capable enough for more tasks at a much lower cost," Arora told CNBC. "Once a model meets the quality bar for the job, that price difference becomes compelling." He added that companies still want frontier U.S. models for their more complicated tasks.

So the market is splitting by task. Engineering teams facing rising inference bills are moving routine and mid-tier work, such as code generation, extraction and agent loops that burn millions of tokens, to cheaper open-weight Chinese models. The hardest reasoning work stays with premium U.S. systems. Because the models are open-weight, companies can also host them on their own infrastructure without sending proprietary data to an outside provider, a point Rest of World has reported comes up in Silicon Valley's debate over the models.

## A Global South story, and a Washington one

The shift is sharpest outside the U.S. and Europe. OpenRouter groups 82 countries in Central and South America, Africa and Asia as the "Global South," and companies there sent **67% of their tokens** to Chinese models in recent weeks. U.S. companies still account for about half of all tokens on OpenRouter. Vercel did not provide a geographic breakdown.

That geography is what worries policy analysts. Daniel Remler, a senior fellow in the technology and national security program at the Center for a New American Security, told CNBC that Chinese AI poses "real economic and security risks for the United States." He described the long-term danger this way: "The ultimate concern is that the integration of Chinese AI models pulls countries into a Chinese technology sphere of influence that hardens into geopolitical alignment." Remler said Southeast Asia, with its close economic ties to China and growing digital infrastructure, could see especially fast adoption.

Two U.S. House committees are now investigating the impact of rising Chinese-model adoption. The probes come as Washington keeps export controls that block Chinese AI companies from buying advanced Nvidia chips, while officials worry those firms could get around the rules by renting compute in overseas data centers or by using distillation, a technique where a new model is trained to imitate an existing one.

## Why It Matters

For years, the business model of frontier AI labs assumed that intelligence would stay scarce and that customers would pay a premium for the best. The OpenRouter and Vercel data challenge that assumption at the margin, which is where most token volume actually sits. Creati.ai put it this way: cheaper competitors could push model providers to compete less on access to scarce intelligence and more on cost, reliability, integration and distribution. A model does not need to beat Claude or GPT across every benchmark to take work away from them. It only has to be good enough for a specific job.

The caveats are real. Token share on developer routing platforms is not the same as share of all AI usage, and it says nothing about enterprise contracts signed directly with OpenAI, Anthropic or Google. U.S. labs still earn more revenue, and a lower API price is not the same as lower total cost once hosting, evaluation, compliance and error correction are included. Still, the direction is clear. The long tail of production AI work is turning into a commodity, and on price, that market currently favors Chinese open-weight models.

The geopolitical side raises the stakes further. If the default AI stack for startups in Lagos, São Paulo and Jakarta is built on Chinese models, the dependencies that follow, such as tooling, fine-tunes, hosting and talent, could last much longer than any single price war.

## What to Watch

The key question is whether token share turns into revenue share. If Chinese providers start winning a meaningful portion of AI spending and not just volume, the pressure on U.S. lab margins becomes concrete. Watch how Anthropic and OpenAI respond on pricing: cheaper tiers, smaller specialized models, and better routing products are the obvious defenses. Watch the two House investigations for any move to restrict U.S. companies from using open-weight Chinese models, which would split the developer market along national lines. And watch whether the Global South pattern Remler described appears in the next round of OpenRouter data, especially in Southeast Asia.
