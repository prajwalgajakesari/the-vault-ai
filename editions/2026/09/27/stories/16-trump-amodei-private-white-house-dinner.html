Trump Confirms Private White House Dinner With Anthropic's Dario Amodei as Relations Thaw

5 min read 967 words 3 sources
KEY TAKEAWAY

Trump hosted Amodei for their first one-on-one dinner, but before the meal he said again that he will not slow AI development, while the Pentagon blacklist upheld on Sept 25 stays in place and no readout has been released.

President Donald Trump sat down to dinner with Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei at the White House on Sunday evening, September 27, the first one-on-one meeting between the two men after a year of open conflict between the administration and one of America's most valuable AI companies. The dinner came just two days after a federal appeals court let the Pentagon keep Anthropic on its blacklist, and hours before Trump publicly repeated that he has no intention of slowing down AI development.

Axios first reported the plan Sunday morning, citing sources familiar with it, and described the meal as a sign of thawing relations between Anthropic and an administration whose officials and allies have had longstanding tensions with the company. Trump later confirmed the dinner to Fox News' Aishah Hasnie. As of Monday, neither the White House nor Anthropic had released a readout of what was discussed.

A Personal Invitation After a Missed State Dinner

The meeting has an unusual backstory. Amodei was the notable absence among the tech leaders who attended Thursday's state dinner for Chinese President Xi Jinping, where OpenAI's Sam Altman, Google's Sundar Pichai, Amazon's Jeff Bezos and Meta's Mark Zuckerberg were among the guests. Axios reported that Amodei had a scheduling conflict, and that Trump personally invited him to the private dinner afterward.

The sit-down also works as a prelude to a larger gathering. Trump and House Speaker Mike Johnson are set to host top AI executives at the White House on Tuesday. "We have a very big meeting on Tuesday where everybody's coming in and that'll be very important," Trump told Hasnie.

A White House official framed the administration's posture in familiar terms. "President Trump has been clear: America will lead the world in Super Intelligence, while protecting American consumers," the official told Axios.

Trump Signals He Won't Budge on Speed

If Anthropic hoped the dinner would soften the president's view on AI risk, Trump's own comments before the meal suggested otherwise. Asked about Amodei's recent call to slow the pace at which AI models improve, Trump said he would discuss it but made clear where he stands.

"We're going to talk about it. I'm for let's go and let's win. We're about a year, maybe a year and a half up on China. There is nobody in third place. It's just us and China," Trump said, according to Fox Business. He added that he had spoken only briefly about the subject with Xi, and argued that opening the issue up to China would mean giving up America's lead.

According to Agence France-Presse and Reuters, as reported by Al Jazeera, Trump acknowledged Amodei's concern that moving too fast could raise risks but said keeping a technological edge over China mattered more. He also took a jab at the Anthropic chief, saying Amodei was trying to be "a perfect little angel" by calling for a pause. "We're leading, and we're building tremendous, trillions of dollars' worth of places. And why should we give that up?" Trump said.

Amodei's position is set out in an essay he published this month. "We must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models. Progress will still seem fast, and we must make wise use of the time we gain," he wrote. Last week he also addressed the U.N. Security Council on the need for international standards to manage fast-moving AI capabilities, Fox Business reported.

A Year of Friction

The dinner lands against a backdrop of escalating hostility. In February, Anthropic refused to let its tools be used for fully autonomous weapons or domestic mass surveillance, and the administration responded by sanctioning the company, AFP and Reuters reported. The Pentagon designated Anthropic a "supply chain risk," which bars the military from using its models.

On Friday, September 25, an appeals court sided with the Pentagon in a 2-1 ruling, allowing the department to keep the designation in place for procurement purposes. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth celebrated on social media: "Confirmed: @AnthropicAI = Supply Chain Risk. The @DeptofWar does what is right for the Country and our Warriors."

The political temperature has been high in other ways too. Axios reported that Trump allies circulated talking points to the White House last week highlighting Amodei's ties to the effective altruism movement. One senior official told the outlet that when Anthropic first floated a one-on-one months ago, some advised against it because "Dario's a little too weird" for Trump. Amodei was also parodied on the most recent episode of Saturday Night Live.

Why It Matters

For Anthropic, face time with the president is worth more than any lobbying campaign. As Axios noted, in an administration so attuned to Trump's moods and personal relationships, a sit-down at the White House is priceless for any company. That is especially true for a firm locked out of defense contracts and on the losing side of a federal appeals ruling.

But the dinner does not resolve the core disagreement. Anthropic's leadership is openly arguing that frontier AI development should slow down, while Trump is framing AI as a two-player race with China that America cannot afford to ease off. A warmer personal relationship could open doors on procurement or regulation, but Trump's pre-dinner remarks suggest any thaw will be about tone rather than policy.

What to Watch

The first test comes Tuesday, when Trump and Speaker Johnson host AI executives at the White House. Whether Amodei attends, and how he is treated alongside rivals like Altman, will show whether Sunday's dinner changed anything. Watch also for any readout from either side, any sign that the Pentagon is reconsidering the supply-chain-risk designation, and whether Anthropic seeks further review of Friday's 2-1 ruling. If the administration keeps the blacklist in place, the dinner may prove more symbolic than substantive.
