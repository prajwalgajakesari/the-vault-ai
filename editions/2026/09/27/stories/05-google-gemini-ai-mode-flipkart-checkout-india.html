Google Tests Direct Flipkart Checkout Inside Gemini and AI Mode in India

5 min read 957 words 4 sources
KEY TAKEAWAY

Google is testing a Flipkart-branded Buy button inside Gemini and AI Mode in India, where Amazon listings get no such button, and plans a wider rollout in October before the festive shopping season.

Google has started letting some shoppers in India buy Flipkart products without leaving Gemini or AI Mode in Search. It is the clearest sign yet that the company wants its AI assistants to close the sale, not just recommend the product. And the first retailer to get the button is one Google partly owns.

The test, first reported by TechCrunch on September 26, puts a "Buy" button on select Flipkart product listings inside Gemini and Google's AI Mode. Tapping it opens a Flipkart-branded checkout flow within the AI interface, according to people familiar with the matter and an experience TechCrunch saw firsthand. For now the experiment covers a small group of users and a narrow set of categories: smartphones, electronics and mobile accessories. Other users still see ordinary Flipkart listings with no direct purchase option.

The scope may not stay small for long. One person familiar with the plans told TechCrunch that Google intends to roll the experience out more broadly later in October, ahead of India's festive shopping season. That is when Flipkart and Amazon run some of their biggest sales of the year.

Google is saying little. A spokesperson told TechCrunch the company is "always testing new features and experiences to help people discover and connect with businesses more easily," and added that Google regularly runs experiments and had no further details to share. Flipkart did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

A Buy Button, but Not the One Google Demoed

The Flipkart flow differs in one important way from what Google has shown before. In January, at the National Retail Federation's Big Show in New York, CEO Sundar Pichai introduced the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP), an open standard meant to let AI agents deal with retailers across discovery, checkout and post-purchase support. Google built it with Shopify, Etsy, Wayfair, Target and Walmart, and more than 20 other companies endorsed it. Google says it works alongside existing agent standards, including Agent2Agent, the Agent Payments Protocol (AP2) and the Model Context Protocol.

"You might wonder why we're introducing another protocol," Pichai told the NRF audience. "The industry needs a protocol that works at global scale and takes into account the nuances of commerce journeys, and this is a critical building block for it."

Pichai said UCP's first use would be native checkout, with a buy button coming to AI Mode and Gemini and purchases completed "with a few taps via Google Pay" inside a Google-hosted experience. The retailer would stay the merchant of record. Since then, Google has added other options to UCP, including handing shoppers off to a retailer's own site to finish buying.

The Flipkart test fits neither description exactly. It shows Flipkart's own branded checkout, not Google's, and it is unclear whether UCP powers it at all. TechCrunch said it could not determine what technology runs the test.

The Walmart Connection

The partnership has deep corporate roots. Walmart owns a majority of Flipkart, and Google put about $350 million into the Indian e-commerce company in 2024 as part of a Walmart-led funding round. That gave Google a minority stake. Walmart was also one of Google's co-developers on UCP, and at NRF it announced that shoppers would be able to buy from Walmart and Sam's Club through Gemini.

"The transition from traditional web or app search to agent-led commerce represents the next great evolution in retail. We aren't just watching the shift, we are driving it," John Furner, then the incoming CEO of Walmart U.S., said at the January keynote, as reported by Retail Rewired.

Earlier this month, Google named Flipkart among the merchants working with it to bring "agentic" shopping to Indian consumers, but it gave no details or timeline. This week's test is the first concrete look at how that partnership reaches shoppers.

Why It Matters

India is the world's second-largest internet market, with more than a billion internet subscribers, and Flipkart and Amazon fight hard for them. In the experience TechCrunch observed, Amazon listings appeared next to Flipkart products but had no Buy button. If that holds through a festive-season rollout, Google's AI surfaces would make one marketplace easier to buy from than its main rival, and Google has a financial stake in that marketplace. That will draw attention from competitors and possibly regulators, because Google's search dominance is already under legal scrutiny in several markets.

The bigger shift is what this means for Google's business. AI Mode and Gemini are replacing pages of blue links with conversational answers, which puts pressure on the ad model that paid for search. Pichai said Google's Shopping Graph holds more than 50 billion product listings, with over 2 billion refreshed every hour. That gives Google the data to recommend products. Turning those recommendations into completed orders would let it keep commercial value as search changes shape. OpenAI is chasing the same prize and has added commerce features, including Walmart shopping, to ChatGPT.

For merchants, the lesson is harsh. When an AI assistant picks what the shopper sees and which listings get one-tap checkout, deep integration becomes a distribution question, not just a technical one.

What to Watch

The first signal is whether the October expansion arrives on schedule, and whether it goes beyond phones and electronics to the categories that drive festive-season volume. Next is whether Google says if the Flipkart flow runs on UCP, since a separate setup would suggest Google is testing several checkout models at once. It also matters whether Amazon or other Indian retailers get the same button, and who handles payments, returns and disputes when an AI-started order goes wrong. Google has so far described this only as an experiment, and those operational details will show whether agentic commerce in India can scale.
