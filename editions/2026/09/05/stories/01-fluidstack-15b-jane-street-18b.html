Fluidstack Doubled Its Valuation in Seven Weeks. Jane Street Wrote the Check.

5 min read 1028 words4 sources
KEY TAKEAWAY

Jane Street led a 1.5 billion dollar round valuing Fluidstack above 18 billion just six and a half weeks after its 7.5 billion disclosure, betting a chip-agnostic data center builder anchored by Anthropic is more defensible than the Nvidia-levered neoclouds.

Seven weeks. That is how long it took Fluidstack — a company that until this summer had never publicly confirmed raising a single dollar of venture capital — to go from a $7.5 billion valuation to more than $18 billion.

On September 3, Forbes reported that the data center builder had closed a $1.5 billion round led by Jane Street Capital, the quantitative trading firm better known for arbitraging bond ETFs than for underwriting transformers and cooling loops. The round more than doubles the valuation Fluidstack disclosed on July 20 and pushes its total funding past $2.6 billion. Its three co-founders, one of them an Oxford dropout, are now billionaires on paper.

The speed is the story. But so is who wrote the check, and what Fluidstack does not own.

The round, and the one before it

Fluidstack spent most of its existence invisible. Founded in 2017 to pool idle GPUs from research labs and gaming rigs — an "Airbnb for graphics cards" — it pivoted after ChatGPT into building dedicated clusters for frontier labs. It announced no funding at all until July 20, 2026, disclosing an $830 million Series A at $7.5 billion led by Situational Awareness, the fund founded by former OpenAI researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner. That round had closed in January. Forbes put the July figure at $750 million; the company says $830 million.

Six and a half weeks later, the number had a two in front of it — the week's second-largest AI financing, behind only Crusoe's $3 billion at $30 billion.

The revenue curve explains the repricing. Fluidstack booked $1.8 million in 2022 and $66.2 million in 2024, the last year covered by UK filings. An investor memo projects roughly $660 million in 2026, more than triple 2025's $200 million, and more than 17 gigawatts of sites by 2030 — exceeding Amazon and Microsoft's combined US footprint today, per Jefferies.

The $50 billion anchor

Everything traces back to November 12, 2025, when Anthropic announced a $50 billion American infrastructure commitment and named Fluidstack — not a hyperscaler — as the partner building custom data centers in Texas and New York. The project should create roughly 800 permanent and 2,400 construction jobs, with sites coming online through 2026.

The pitch was speed. Conventional multi-gigawatt facilities take 18 to 36 months; Fluidstack assembles them from factory-built modules and targets roughly six.

"Fluidstack was built for this moment," said Gary Wu, co-founder and CEO, when the deal was announced. "We are proud to partner with frontier AI leaders like Anthropic to accelerate and deploy the infrastructure necessary to realize their vision."

Anthropic framed it as a capability constraint, not a real estate deal. "We are getting closer to AI that can accelerate scientific discovery and help solve complex problems in ways that were not possible before," said CEO Dario Amodei. "These sites will help us build more capable AI systems that can drive those breakthroughs, while creating American jobs."

Why Jane Street

Jane Street is not a tourist here. The firm generated $16.1 billion in trading revenue in the first quarter of 2026, much of it on AI-driven strategies. In April it signed a $6 billion CoreWeave cloud agreement and took a $1 billion stake in the company. It has backed Anthropic repeatedly, at a valuation now above $800 billion.

Leading Fluidstack closes a triangle: Jane Street now holds positions in the model company, the chip-cloud vendor and the facility builder, while remaining one of the world's largest private buyers of compute. Conspicuously absent from the cap table is Nvidia, which has seeded CoreWeave, Nebius, Nscale and Lambda with billions.

Analysis: the neocloud that owns no chips

That absence is the most interesting thing about this valuation. CoreWeave and Nebius — worth roughly $47 billion and $57.4 billion in public markets — buy Nvidia GPUs and rent them out; their multiples are levered to Nvidia continuing to win. Fluidstack builds the buildings, secures the power and runs the software: its bare-metal Atlas OS and Lighthouse monitoring layer, chip-agnostic across Nvidia H100 through GB200 and AMD MI300X and MI355X. The silicon belongs to the customer. That sidesteps GPU depreciation, the quiet margin killer for chip-owning neoclouds, whose bare-metal gross margins McKinsey pegs at 14 to 16 percent.

Which is where Google enters. Forbes reports Fluidstack appears to be the first publicly known operator of TPU data centers outside Google itself — the proving ground for Google's effort to turn its tensor processing units into a credible Nvidia rival. Google has also acted as Fluidstack's balance sheet, backstopping $3.2 billion of its lease obligations at TeraWulf's Lake Mariner campus in New York (about 360 MW) and $1.73 billion at Cipher Mining's Barber Lake site in Texas (about 207 MW), taking equity in both former bitcoin miners in exchange.

Read together: Anthropic and Google are jointly financing an independent operator that reduces both companies' dependence on Nvidia and on each other's clouds. Anthropic already splits workloads across AWS Trainium, Google TPUs and now Fluidstack. Compute diversification has become a strategic posture, not a procurement detail.

The risk runs the other way. Roughly $50 billion of Fluidstack's contracted revenue flows from one customer, and with no chip inventory to redeploy, a slowdown at Anthropic has nowhere to go.

What to watch

Execution, and politics. Data Center Watch counted at least 75 US projects worth about $130 billion blocked or delayed in the first quarter of 2026 alone, with opposition groups more than doubling from 396 to 833. Fluidstack's ten-site buildout sits squarely in that environment.

"The neocloud model works brilliantly when demand is surging and capital is cheap," said Patrick Moorhead, CEO and chief analyst at Moor Insights and Strategy. "The real test comes when you have to deliver physical infrastructure at scale while managing supply chains, permitting, and community relations."

Watch three things: whether the first Anthropic sites energize on schedule in 2026, whether Google converts its backstops into equity after February reports of a $100 million investment talk, and whether Fluidstack files to go public before private rounds run out of headroom. At this pace, the next repricing may arrive before the concrete cures.
