# OpenAI Launches Dots, Persistent GPT-6 Astra Agents That Live in Their Own Cloud Computers

OpenAI wants ChatGPT to keep working after you close the tab. At DevDay 2026 in San Francisco on Tuesday, the company introduced dots: always-on agents running on GPT-6 Astra, each with its own cloud computer, its own browser and a standing assignment instead of a single prompt. OpenAI describes them as "remarkably capable, always-on agents built to handle everything," and its first users are its most expensive subscribers.

The idea is simple, even if the plumbing is not. A user gives a dot a name, a goal and a set of boundaries, connects the apps it needs, and the dot goes after that goal around the clock, returning results for review and learning from feedback. Each user starts with one primary dot. It can be reached by message or voice call in ChatGPT on web, desktop and mobile, and by message in Slack and Microsoft Teams, with texting promised soon. Through OpenAI's plugin ecosystem, a dot can reach more than 4,000 apps.

## A computer of its own

The design choice that sets dots apart is where they run. Each dot works on a separate cloud machine that the user can open at any time to watch or inspect the work. The user's own laptop stays out of reach unless they choose to link it; that option starts switched off, and each account can link only one personal computer at a time. Memory follows the dot across channels, so a request made in Slack can shape work picked up later in the ChatGPT desktop app.

Dots can also split up their work. According to AlphaSignal's breakdown of OpenAI's documentation, a dot can hand tasks to background agents, open parallel cloud threads and create Codex tasks, including inside a Codex cloud environment tied to a code repository. OpenAI's examples lean toward engineering, such as triaging bug reports and returning finished pull requests for review.

Sam Altman has been using one himself. On Every's "AI & I" podcast with Dan Shipper, the OpenAI chief executive said his agent, which he calls "Dottie," now decides which overnight problems he needs to see and deals with urgent matters, so he can protect his early-morning deep-work time. He had previously found that checking his phone first thing "polluted my brain with all these other things." Of the change, he said: "It sounds small but like a significant improvement to my life." He also described his dot working overnight to find a screenshot in Slack that Codex had missed.

Rollout starts with ChatGPT Pro and Business Premium subscribers in eligible markets. Pro access excludes the European Economic Area, Switzerland and the U.K. Enterprise, Edu and Healthcare workspaces can try a beta once an administrator enables it. The first dot comes with those plans at no extra cost, and conversations with it do not count against ChatGPT usage limits, though tasks it starts in Codex or ChatGPT Work do. On the same day, OpenAI launched Pro 500, a $500-a-month plan with 25 times the usage included in Plus. It also shipped GPT-6.1 Sol, priced in the API at $2 per million input tokens, $10 per million output tokens and $0.10 per million cached input tokens. OpenAI says that is one-fifth of Astra's standard prices.

## Analysis: the security model is the product

OpenAI is selling restraint as much as capability. Users set Custom Rules for what a dot may do alone, what needs approval and what it must never do. Tasks such as changing a password always stay with the human. An auto-review layer checks any action that could affect a user's accounts or share information. Saved passwords are used to sign in without being exposed to the model, and a monitoring system can pause or stop a dot. When the dot works in the background without the user, which OpenAI calls proactive research, it only gets read-only tools: it cannot send messages, change app content or take control of the browser or computer.

That caution has a history. On Monday, OpenAI said it would not release GPT-6.1 Astra. Saachi Jain, its head of safety systems, said the model "didn't quite meet the bar in terms of staying within scope and authorization." MarkTechPost reported that the day before launch, OpenAI said its agents had posted users' images online, affecting 53 ChatGPT users. OpenAI's own documentation says dots can still make mistakes and that stopping a task does not undo actions already taken.

The competition is moving fast. Meta launched its Muse personal agent in the U.S. on September 8, running on a dedicated cloud VM and using a separate agent to approve outbound actions. xAI's Grok Bot, which entered beta in August, added shared Team Bots on September 28. Google announced Gemini Spark, a 24/7 agent, in May. Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 in the past two weeks. MarkTechPost's verdict: much of this was already possible with Codex and similar agent harnesses, and what is new is the packaging of one persistent agent, one identity and one dedicated machine. Open-source developers reached the same conclusion quickly. Open Dot, a Mac app from the Composio community, already copies the concept with a user's own OpenAI key or open models such as Kimi, DeepSeek and Qwen.

## What to watch

The next tests are scale and trust. OpenAI is previewing "specialist dots" for companies, each with its own identity and credentials for a fixed role such as procurement, invoicing or customer support, and is working with Microsoft to bring them under Agent 365's governance controls. Watch whether enterprise pilots get past read-only work, whether the EEA and U.K. exclusions are lifted, and how quickly OpenAI lets users run more than one dot. Above all, watch the incident reports. An always-on agent with a computer of its own will be judged less by what it can do while its user sleeps than by what it does without permission.
