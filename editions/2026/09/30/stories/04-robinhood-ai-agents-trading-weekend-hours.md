# Robinhood Hands Trading to AI Agents and Plans Weekend Stock Hours, Pushing Agents Directly Into Money Movement

Robinhood wants an AI agent sitting in every trader's account, and it wants that agent able to trade at any hour, weekends included. At its HOOD Summit in Houston on Tuesday night, the brokerage unveiled Robinhood Agents, an AI system built into its app that can research markets, build strategies and place trades on a customer's behalf. It also said that, pending regulatory review, it plans to let customers trade a selection of U.S. stocks and ETFs 24 hours a day, seven days a week.

"Ownership doesn't work without markets, and markets don't work without traders," said Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev. "We're making Robinhood the best place in the world for active traders by delivering tools once reserved for hedge funds, big banks, and quant firms."

## From developer toy to mainstream product

Robinhood has been testing the idea since May, when it opened its platform to outside AI agents through a Model Context Protocol (MCP) server aimed at technically minded customers. The company says more than 150,000 customers have opened agentic trading accounts since then, and that agents now call Robinhood's tools almost 30 million times a day.

The new product removes most of that setup. A customer names an agent, opens a dedicated agentic account and picks a model. CoinDesk reported that the choices include models from OpenAI and Anthropic, and Robinhood said usage of OpenAI's GPT-Luna will be free until the end of the year. The agent can then analyze markets and trade stocks, options and crypto. A forthcoming feature called Loops will turn a strategy into a standing instruction the agent carries out on repeat, such as checking the market every morning or running an overnight strategy while the user sleeps.

Robinhood is also opening an in-app store of paid data sources for agents, called Agent Apps. It lists 11 providers, including Unusual Whales, Nasdaq, SpotGamma, Quiver Quantitative and Token Terminal, with subscriptions from $5 to $30 a month after a one-month free trial.

"With safety and security at its core, we're setting the standard for what agentic finance can be," said Abhishek Fatehpuria, Robinhood's vice president of product management.

The weekend plan builds on the Robinhood 24 Hour Market, which launched in 2023 and runs from Sunday at 8 p.m. ET to Friday at 8 p.m. ET. Weekend trading will start with a curated list of stocks and ETFs and will run on Bruce ATS, an alternative trading system. "Breaking news doesn't wait for an opening bell," said Steve Quirk, Robinhood's chief brokerage officer.

The rest of the lineup leans just as hard into active trading. Robinhood announced crypto perpetual futures through Bitstamp, with up to 10x leverage on bitcoin and ether and 3x on other tokens; binary earnings contracts listed on Cboe; options trading hours that will run from 7:30 a.m. to 4:15 p.m. starting in October; and 4x intraday margin for eligible customers, up from 2x.

Wall Street liked the pace. "HOOD delivered the two key products we were watching, Agents and U.S. perps, while broader active-trader enhancements add more ways for customers to trade," Morgan Stanley analyst Michael Cyprys wrote in a note, according to Investing.com. He reiterated an Overweight rating and a $150 price target.

## Why it matters: agents that move real money

This agent is pointed squarely at a brokerage account. That makes Robinhood's rollout one of the clearest tests yet of what happens when a general-purpose language model is given authority over money, and is allowed to use it at 3 a.m. on a Sunday.

Robinhood's main safeguard is containment. An agent can only touch the funds in its dedicated agentic account, and CoinDesk reported that agents cannot borrow on margin at launch. Customers are asked during setup whether each trade needs manual approval, and that setting is on by default. It can also be switched off. Loops, by Robinhood's own description, may place, modify or cancel trades without asking for approval each time.

The company's executives describe the agent as an assistant, not a replacement for the customer's judgment. "I would think of the agent as an extension, like a trading tool that's mainly for research that can execute trades," Gina Pasqua-Abeles, a senior director of product management, told CoinDesk. "We do expect users to still have some level of oversight over what their agent is doing."

The legal fine print puts the risk squarely on the customer. Robinhood's disclosures say users assume all risk for trades executed by AI agents, and that the company "does not control, supervise, monitor, recommend, or audit agents." The agent software comes from a separate subsidiary, Robinhood Labs, which the company says is not a broker-dealer or investment adviser. That structure will likely draw regulators' attention, because it separates the entity making trading decisions from the entities that are licensed to execute them.

Pairing agents with round-the-clock markets adds to that pressure. Weekend sessions tend to be thinner, and Reuters has reported concerns that lower liquidity outside normal hours can leave investors with worse prices. An agent that keeps trading through a quiet Saturday, reacting to a headline before most humans are awake, is exactly the scenario where weak liquidity and automated decisions can compound each other. Robinhood is not alone here: NYSE and Nasdaq have both filed to extend their hours.

## What to watch

Weekend equities still need regulatory sign-off, and Robinhood has not given a firm launch date beyond saying the feature is coming. The more revealing test will come once Robinhood Agents and Loops reach eligible U.S. customers at scale. Watch how many users switch trade approvals off, how the agents behave during the first sharp weekend price swing, and whether the SEC or FINRA weigh in on who is responsible when an AI agent's trade goes wrong. If the bet pays off, rivals such as Schwab, Interactive Brokers and Fidelity may feel pressure to follow.
