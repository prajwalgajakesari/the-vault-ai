ElevenLabs Doubles to a $22B Valuation in $300M Employee Tender as Voice Agents Hit 55% of Revenue

5 min read 985 words 4 sources
KEY TAKEAWAY

ElevenLabs' $300M employee tender at $22B, double its February valuation, shows investors now value it as a full-stack enterprise voice-agent platform rather than a text-to-speech vendor.

Seven months after investors valued it at $11 billion, ElevenLabs is now worth twice that. The London-based voice AI company said on Wednesday that it has closed a $300 million employee tender offer at a $22 billion valuation. Wellington and T. Rowe Price led the deal, and the company says demand for its conversational agents is driving the growth. Enterprise customers deploying those agents now account for 55% of its revenue.

The deal is a secondary sale, not a new fundraising round. In a tender offer, employees and existing shareholders sell their shares to incoming investors, which gives them liquidity without the company necessarily raising new capital. Even so, the price sets a new benchmark for one of Europe's most valuable AI startups. ElevenLabs raised $500 million in a primary Series D in February at an $11 billion valuation. A year ago, in September 2025, it ran a $100 million employee tender at $6.6 billion. Its first round in 2022 valued it at $9 million.

Who bought in, and why

The investor list is long. EQT, Goldman Sachs, GIC, the Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan, Sapphire Ventures and BDT & MSD invested in ElevenLabs for the first time. Existing backers including Andreessen Horowitz, Lightspeed, ICONIQ, D.E. Shaw, Evantic, DISRUPTIVE and Alkeon also took part. ElevenLabs called the two lead investors "long-term institutional investors with the experience our next stage of growth requires." Co-founder and chief executive Mati Staniszewski, who started the company in 2022 with Piotr Dabkowski, announced the close on X. In a statement, he cast the deal as a bet on how people will interact with AI rather than on synthetic speech alone.

"AI should interact with people the way we interact with each other," Staniszewski said. "We're already seeing rapid adoption of expressive voice agents by enterprises and governments, who are deploying them in service of consumers and citizens. As we grow, it's important to us to let our people share in some of the value they are creating for our customers and the wider world."

The company now has more than 800 employees and says it is committed to giving them "regular opportunities for liquidity."

The agent business behind the number

Most of the valuation jump comes from ElevenAgents, the company's platform for deploying voice and chat agents that connect to a customer's own knowledge and systems. ElevenLabs says it is its fastest-growing product. According to the company, those agents now handle more than 15 million conversations a week, three times the February level. They process refunds and exchanges, renew insurance policies, upgrade phone plans, book healthcare appointments and help people access public services. ElevenAgents' annual recurring revenue has also more than tripled since the Series D, the company said, though it did not disclose a dollar figure.

Since February, customers including Stripe, Deutsche Telekom, DoorDash's SevenRooms, Admiral, Customers Bank and Cadence have rolled out the platform, as have the governments of Ukraine and Greece. ElevenLabs says its technology is used in daily operations at five of the world's ten largest tech companies, five of the ten largest insurers and four of the ten largest telecoms. It also cited an internal analysis of its customers that found voice agents resolve issues 31% faster on average than chat agents. The number of companies running multichannel support through ElevenAgents has doubled since February.

The research side is still producing new models. ElevenLabs released Eleven v4 and v4 Turbo, which it calls its fastest and most emotive text-to-speech models, alongside Scribe v2 Medical, a transcription model built for healthcare. Its models speak, listen and translate in more than 90 languages, which the company says are spoken natively by over 5.5 billion people.

T. Rowe Price, one of the lead investors, pointed to that vertical integration as the reason it backed the company. "As ElevenLabs has grown its conversational agent business, the combination of research, product, and deployment has proved an important structural advantage," said Emma Norchet at T. Rowe Price. "The team is able to make improvements at every layer of the stack, allowing them to execute at high velocity and deliver immediate value for its enterprise clients."

Why it matters

The deal shows that investors are now paying for AI agents, not for the models underneath them. ElevenLabs built its reputation on human-sounding text-to-speech, and that kind of capability is quickly becoming a commodity as big labs ship their own voice models. The company now presents itself as a full-stack "interaction platform." That means owning the models, the agent product, and teams of forward-deployed engineers who tailor deployments to specific industries such as finance, healthcare, government, retail and telecom. Its argument is that voice depends heavily on language, culture and brand, so generic models from horizontal providers will struggle in regulated, customer-facing work.

ElevenLabs is also not alone in the market. Some of the biggest AI investments this year have gone to agent developers as businesses automate customer service and routine back-office work. Large tech companies are moving in too: Meta has been expanding its Muse personal agent to small businesses. A $22 billion private valuation, backed by public-market investors, suggests the market believes a specialist can hold its ground against those platforms. It also puts more pressure on ElevenLabs to keep growing at its current pace.

What to watch

The next milestone is whether ElevenLabs turns its momentum with crossover investors into a primary raise or a path to a public listing. It also has not yet disclosed hard revenue figures for ElevenAgents, beyond saying ARR has more than tripled. Watch for whether Eleven v4 keeps its lead on independent benchmarks, whether more government deals follow Ukraine and Greece, and how its enterprise share holds up as Meta and other large platforms push their own agents into the same customer-service budgets.
