Claude for Government Goes GA in FedRAMP High, With No Seat Fees and Hard Spending Caps for Agencies

5 min read 994 words 5 sources
KEY TAKEAWAY

Anthropic made Claude for Government generally available in FedRAMP High with prepaid, hard-capped usage and no seat fees, betting on civilian and state agencies while a D.C. Circuit ruling keeps it shut out of the Pentagon.

Five days after a federal appeals court let the Pentagon keep Anthropic on its blacklist, the company opened its doors wider to the rest of the government. On Wednesday, Anthropic made Claude for Government generally available to U.S. federal and state agencies, moving the FedRAMP High authorized platform out of a public beta that began in July. It is pitching agencies on a pricing model built for appropriations law rather than enterprise IT: no per-seat fees, prepaid usage, and a spending ceiling that cannot be crossed.

"Agencies pay for usage in fixed increments with a hard not-to-exceed cap, so spend does not exceed what an agency has obligated," Anthropic wrote in its launch announcement. For federal budget officers, an open-ended metered AI bill is a non-starter. Administrators can define user tiers with spend and model limits per group, track usage by user and by model, and receive burndown alerts before a prepaid balance runs low.

What agencies actually get

The GA release delivers Claude's chat, file-handling and agentic capabilities through the Claude desktop app, alongside Claude Code for software teams. Anthropic says agencies get capabilities comparable to its commercial customers, with new features generally arriving on the commercial release cadence. Anthropic cites memo drafting, RFP reviews, casework and legacy-system modernization as target uses.

The administrative model mirrors how departments are organized. Department-level administrators allocate prepaid usage to sub-agencies, each of which manages its own users. Agencies connect their own identity provider for single sign-on, and SCIM group mappings set rate limits, dollar caps and allowed models for each seat tier. Layered configuration lets parent departments set defaults for sub-agencies, including which features are available and what Claude can connect to.

Oversight features are aimed squarely at authorizing officials and inspectors general. Administrative actions are written to an audit log, and sensitive operations on Anthropic's side require two-person approval. Usage exports contain metering data only, which Anthropic says lets agencies answer ATO and IG requests without moving sensitive material. Conversation history stays local on the agency-managed device, and the app deploys through standard agency mobile device management platforms. No separate cloud-provider relationship is required.

Two more products are entering early access inside the same boundary and under the same controls: the Claude Code command-line interface and Claude for Microsoft 365. The GA had been telegraphed. At the Billington Cybersecurity Summit on Sept. 9, Teresa Carlson, Anthropic's newly hired global head of public sector, said the desktop product would move to general availability within weeks and that the company's Fable 5.1 model was already on the platform, meaning "any agency that requires FedRAMP High can use it."

A government customer split in two

The launch lands in an awkward week for Anthropic in Washington. On Sept. 25, a divided D.C. Circuit panel upheld the Pentagon's designation of Anthropic as a supply chain risk under the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act, a label that bars the military and its contractors from using Claude. Writing for the 2-1 majority, Judge Gregory Katsas said, "On more than one occasion, these restrictions have stopped Claude from performing tasks requested by government users." The dispute traces back to Anthropic's refusal to drop contractual limits on lethal autonomous warfare and mass domestic surveillance. Judge Karen Henderson dissented, and an Anthropic spokesperson said the company was weighing its next steps.

Yet civilian government has kept buying. Anthropic's $1-per-user OneGov deal through the General Services Administration was recently extended to Oct. 31, and on Sept. 29 Dario Amodei was among the executives who signed the White House's voluntary Accord on Super Intelligence alongside leaders from OpenAI, Google, Meta, xAI and Nvidia. The GA is a bet that civilian and state agencies are market enough while the defense fight plays out.

Analysis: the procurement race shifts from promos to plumbing

For the past year, the federal AI race has been fought largely on headline prices through GSA's OneGov program. That phase is ending. The introductory deals were set to expire Sept. 30, and the vendors have now diverged. OpenAI struck a new OneGov agreement running through 2028 that, according to FedScoop, waives ChatGPT's license fee and discounts usage by 50 percent. Google extended its $0.47-per-agency Gemini for Government deal to Nov. 15. xAI has offered Grok to agencies for 42 cents and is pursuing FedRAMP High with USDA sponsorship, while the Pentagon, freed of Anthropic, has moved to deploy Grok internally.

GSA is framing the churn as healthy. "We welcome additional AI technology companies to engage with GSA and explore opportunities to bring their capabilities to agencies through OneGov as we continue to expand choice, increase competition, and deliver value for taxpayers," Federal Acquisition Service acting Commissioner Laura Stanton said. GSA says OneGov has produced nearly $1.7 billion in cost savings, $1.4 billion of it from AI tools reaching about 3.5 million federal employees.

Anthropic's GA reads as a pivot from that promotional race toward durable infrastructure. Per-seat giveaways win pilots; prepaid caps, sub-agency allocation, SCIM tiers and metering-only exports are what survive budget cycles and ATO reviews. OpenAI's usage-based, no-license-fee structure suggests the two leading labs are converging on the same thesis: government AI will be bought like a utility with a hard ceiling, not like a software subscription. Google leans on its Workspace and Cloud footprint, xAI on its Pentagon relationship; Anthropic is staking its claim on agentic coding and governance controls.

What to watch next: whether Anthropic seeks further review of the D.C. Circuit ruling, and how that interacts with the parallel California decision that went its way; whether its OneGov arrangement is rewritten or lapses after Oct. 31; and how quickly the Claude Code CLI and Microsoft 365 integrations exit early access. Carlson has also said Mythos and Opus are headed to classified environments, a test of how far Anthropic can go while the Pentagon keeps it out.
