Weeks before Anthropic is expected to make its public-market debut, a memo making the rounds inside the White House is trying to recast the company's chief executive as something far more politically toxic than a cautious technologist. The document, written by a Trump political adviser and obtained by Axios, paints Dario Amodei as the face of AI "doomerism" and a founding figure of effective altruism, the philanthropic movement the memo accuses of having "built the AI-doom pipeline."

The timing is hard to miss. The memo surfaced on September 24, one day after Amodei addressed the United Nations Security Council by video link and warned that advanced AI, if handled badly, could endanger everyone. It also lands as Anthropic prepares what is widely expected to be a record-setting IPO, reportedly pushed from October into November, with the company running at a roughly $100 billion revenue pace.

## What the Memo Says

According to Axios reporters Maria Curi and Marc Caputo, the memo seeks to portray effective altruism, or EA, as a fringe, cultish collective out of step with mainstream America. It was drafted with President Trump in mind, though its author does not work for the White House. The document argues that the movement prioritizes "foreigners over citizens, shrimp over families, future hypothetical people over the living, and — on the current agenda — possible machine minds over Americans."

The memo names Dario Amodei as one of the people who "built the [EA] network," and it extends the charge to his sister, Anthropic president Daniela Amodei, whose husband once led a philanthropy focused on AI safety. The document bundles those family and professional ties under a single label: "The Anthropic knot." It also lists a series of positions calculated to alarm conservative readers, including abortion, veganism and granting civil-rights-style protections to "digital minds," though Axios noted the memo does not directly tie those ideas to Amodei. It does, however, make a point of mentioning Sam Bankman-Fried, the disgraced FTX founder who remains EA's most notorious association.

The sentiment is echoed by people close to the administration. "Amodei is the embodiment of an ideology and globalist approach to innovation that's counter to the president's America First agenda," one source close to the administration told Axios. A senior White House official told the outlet that months ago, when Anthropic staff asked about a meeting between Amodei and the president, officials advised against it because "Dario's a little too weird" for Trump.

## Anthropic's Side of the Story

Anthropic has spent considerable effort distancing itself from the EA label, and nothing in the public record shows the company responding directly to the memo as of publication. The White House also had not publicly addressed the document. The company's existing position is clear, however. In a 2025 Wired profile, Daniela Amodei said of effective altruism, "I don't identify with that terminology." Early employee Amanda Askell, who helps shape Claude's constitution, has said EA is not a theme of the organization, and Dario Amodei has repeatedly said in interviews that he is not a member of the movement while also publicly touting AI's benefits.

The memo's target is also not the only voice urging caution. Axios pointed out that OpenAI's Sam Altman, Google DeepMind chair Demis Hassabis and Elon Musk have all recently called for slowing AI development after a string of incidents in which AI agents hacked outside companies, and that AI safety has become a bipartisan issue in Congress. Criticism of EA also cuts across party lines; one Democratic donor and tech entrepreneur told Axios the movement's beliefs are "crazy" and "dangerous."

## The UN Backdrop

The memo follows a pointed exchange at the UN on September 23. Speaking to the Security Council, Amodei said, "if managed poorly, I even believe that AI could be a risk to humanity as a whole," and pressed for narrow international rules, beginning with a ban on AI-designed bioweapons, according to CNN. He also said Anthropic would be willing to slow development. At the same session, White House Office of Science and Technology Policy director Michael Kratsios dismissed such governance efforts as a "globalist scheme," language that closely mirrors the framing in the adviser's memo.

## Why It Matters

This is a political document, not a policy, and it originates outside the government. But its circulation inside the White House signals that Anthropic's already rocky relationship with the administration may be heading into another rough stretch, just as the company asks public investors to buy in. Axios reported that some in the administration had set aside earlier grievances largely because Anthropic leads the AI frontier; if rivals such as OpenAI close that gap, the incentive to tolerate the company shrinks.

For investors and enterprise buyers, the question is how much political exposure comes attached to Anthropic's safety-first identity. Federal procurement, export policy and regulatory posture all run through an administration whose allies are now openly framing the CEO's worldview as opposed to America First. Amodei's safety warnings are central to Anthropic's brand; the memo attempts to turn that brand into a liability.

## What to Watch

The clearest signal will come in Anthropic's IPO filing, and whether its risk-factor disclosures expand to address political and regulatory hostility. Watch too for whether the memo's language migrates from an outside adviser's document into official statements from OSTP or the West Wing, whether any senior official publicly endorses it, and whether Anthropic tones down Amodei's public safety messaging ahead of the roadshow or doubles down on it.
