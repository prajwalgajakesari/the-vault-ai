# Instinct Raises $1B Series C at $10B Valuation to Build Personal AI Agents for Everyone

Four weeks ago, Instinct was a viral curiosity valued at $2.5 billion. On Monday it became one of the most valuable consumer AI startups in the world. The San Francisco company, which builds a personal AI agent you reach by text or phone call, announced a $1 billion Series C from Sequoia Capital, Benchmark Capital and Coatue at a $10 billion valuation. That quadruples its price in about a month, for a product that is still invite-only, has no mobile app, and has not shared a single growth metric.

The round was first reported by The Information earlier in September and confirmed in a Business Wire release on September 28. It comes barely a month after Instinct told The Wall Street Journal on August 26 that it had raised a $250 million Series B, co-led by Index Ventures and Benchmark, at a $2.5 billion valuation. That round brought total funding to $350 million, and the new money pushes the total past $1.3 billion. Benchmark is the one investor to appear in both rounds.

"We're building Instinct to be the best personal agent that can handle the deeply personal nuances of everyday life," founder Noah Shinn said in the announcement. "This funding helps us bring Instinct to more people and continue building the future of personal AI. It's an exciting, creative time, and we're just getting started." Instinct did not make Shinn available for interviews, TechCrunch reported.

## An agent with its own phone and computer

Instinct is legally Spear Street Technology Inc., a company Shinn founded in 2025. According to the Journal's reporting, as summarized by PYMNTS, it began a private beta in February and took off in early August after users started posting about what it could do. The pitch is simple. Instead of giving you a new app or interface, Instinct works over SMS and voice. You text or call it, and the agent finishes the job using a virtual phone and computer of its own. The company says it can "use devices the same way humans do," SiliconANGLE reported, which puts it in the computer-use camp: it loads web pages and apps and clicks through them the way a person would.

Instinct's examples are errands most people put off: planning a cross-country road trip, ordering the weekly groceries, canceling forgotten subscriptions, arranging a ride to the airport, booking a handyman. Alongside the funding, it detailed several features released in recent months. Instinct Concierge is a "white-glove" service for high-touch jobs such as phoning a restaurant that doesn't take online reservations, getting on a dentist's cancellation list, or sorting out a cable bill. The Trusted Person Network runs on what the company calls an Instinct-to-Instinct communication protocol. It lets one user's agent coordinate plans directly with friends' agents and swap PDFs, images and spreadsheets. Location sharing over iMessage lets the assistant notice when a user arrives somewhere and make suggestions.

Instinct hasn't published usage numbers. The Information reported on September 15 that its invitation-only user base had passed 100,000. That figure puts the new valuation at roughly $100,000 per reported user, a price that only makes sense if investors expect the waitlist to turn into a mass-market product.

## The privacy bill comes due

These capabilities depend on broad access. Unite.AI's review of Instinct's privacy policy, last revised August 26, found that depending on the permissions granted, the agent may access message and email content, audio, payment information, third-party usernames and passwords, and health-related data. Under its terms of service, users appoint the service as their agent to enter agreements and transactions on their behalf. The terms also warn that Actions "may not always be reversible." The company's first privacy policy drew sharp criticism in August, TechCrunch noted, and has since been rewritten. On Monday, Instinct emphasized isolated sandboxes, short-lived local credentials, identity-signed tool execution, and a new "active detective" system that it says catches subtle hallucinations before the agent acts.

## Why It Matters

Instinct's rise is the clearest evidence yet that the next consumer AI race is about agents that act, not chatbots that answer. It is also a bet against the platforms. Meta's Muse agent, launched in early September, offers many of the same features plus deep hooks into Instagram DMs, Facebook Groups and Marketplace. TechCrunch reports it has reached the top of the U.S. app stores with millions of downloads. SiliconANGLE counts roughly a dozen serious contenders, including startups Wajo, Ollie and Poke and agentic versions of ChatGPT, Grok and Apple's Siri, which was rebuilt this year on Apple Intelligence. OpenAI may be next: code strings found in ChatGPT point to an always-on assistant called "o," and leaks suggest a launch as soon as September 29, though OpenAI has not confirmed it.

Against that field, Instinct's advantages are speed and focus. It has no legacy app to protect and no ad business pulling on its incentives, and the SMS-first design means it works on any phone. Its weaknesses are the same things. It doesn't own a platform, a distribution channel or an operating system, and every task runs through someone else's website, phone line or messaging app. A $1 billion war chest buys time and compute. It does not buy the default position that Meta, Apple and possibly OpenAI can give themselves.

## What to Watch

The next test is access: Instinct now has to move from a waitlist of reportedly 100,000-plus early adopters to the "everyone" in its fundraising pitch, probably with a native app and paid tiers. Watch whether OpenAI's rumored "o" ships this week and how its pricing compares, whether Muse's concierge-call feature goes wide, and whether regulators start looking at consumer agents that hold users' passwords and can sign agreements on their behalf. The first time an agent makes a costly mistake that can't be undone, the whole category will feel the impact, and Instinct will be the most visible target.
