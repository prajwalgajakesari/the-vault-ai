# Anthropic's IPO Prospectus Lays Bare the Cost of Frontier AI: $4.6B Revenue, $42B Loss, and a Possible $2T Valuation

Anthropic took in $4.59 billion in revenue last year and posted a net loss nearly nine times that size. That is the central finding of the Claude maker's IPO prospectus, which Reuters reviewed and reported on Monday. The filing gives public investors their first audited look at what it costs to build frontier AI, and it asks them to weigh that cost against a listing that could value the company at more than $2 trillion.

The revenue growth is hard to argue with. Sales rose from $386 million in 2024 to $4.59 billion in 2025, an increase of roughly 1,088%, or about twelvefold. Costs rose too. Operating expenses reached $12.65 billion, and $7.33 billion of that went to compute and infrastructure, about 58% of the total and roughly triple the 2024 figure. The result was an operating loss of $8.06 billion, up from $2.98 billion a year earlier.

The headline net loss of $41.97 billion needs explaining. According to Reuters, about $34 billion of it is a non-cash charge reflecting the higher estimated value of financing instruments that could eventually convert into shares. No cash left the building for that portion, though it signals dilution. The $8 billion operating deficit is the better gauge of how the business performs.

## Half a Trillion in Commitments

The largest number in the document is not a loss. The prospectus lists about $518 billion in cloud, computing and infrastructure obligations. Reuters corrected its first report to say those obligations fall due over "the coming years," not the coming year, and some early write-ups still carry the uncorrected wording. Even spread over several years, the figure is more than 70 times what Anthropic spent on compute in all of 2025. It shows how much capacity the company has already agreed to buy on the bet that demand will keep compounding.

The skeptics spoke up quickly. Ross Hendricks, an equity analyst and author of the Ross Report, doubted the financing could be found. "Just a casual mention of raising half a trillion in a single year, really? Does anyone believe this?" he wrote on X, according to Stocktwits. Ed Zitron, the AI critic and chief executive of EZ Primary Research, focused on the income statement: "Spent $12.6bn to make $4.6bn in revenue in 2025, $7.33bn of which was compute costs. Operating loss of $8bn. Losses getting worse."

On the other side of the ledger, Anthropic ended 2025 with $20.28 billion in cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments. The filing also discloses heavy customer concentration. Two customers each accounted for about 12% of sales, so the pair made up nearly a quarter of revenue. In its risk factors, the company warns that many large clients are not bound by long-term contracts and could cut or stop their spending.

## A Company Built to Resist Its Shareholders

The prospectus is also unusual in how it handles governance. A separate Reuters report on the filing says Anthropic's seven co-founders, including CEO Dario Amodei and President Daniela Amodei, will hold control through a new "Founder LLC" backed by a high-vote share class. The filing describes the co-founders as "distinctly equipped to be stewards of our mission." A member can be removed for quitting, dying, selling too many shares, or for "cause." The super-voting class begins to sunset once two or fewer co-founders or their successors remain. The co-founders also pledged 80% of their personal Anthropic equity to charitable causes.

The filing says plainly that this setup may cost investors. It warns that the structure could produce decisions "that may conflict with short-, medium-, or long-term financial interests and business performance, which may negatively impact the value of our Class A common stock." It also gives an example of revenue the company has chosen to forgo: "we have chosen not to develop certain commercially attractive offerings, such as image and video generation models, in order to direct our compute toward our research and safety priorities."

## Why It Matters

Until now, the finances of frontier AI labs have been pieced together from leaks, investor letters and run-rate claims. Anthropic's prospectus is the first full set of numbers from a top-tier lab, and it will serve as the benchmark for every AI listing that follows, OpenAI's included. The main takeaway is that the product sells but serving it is very expensive. Anthropic spent $1.60 on compute alone for every dollar of 2025 revenue, before salaries or research. The bull case is that revenue is growing much faster than costs, and secondary reporting on the filing points to a sharply higher run rate in 2026. The bear case is the $518 billion in commitments. They lock in spending whether or not demand holds up, and they come alongside a customer base where two buyers supply nearly a quarter of sales.

The governance design raises a second question for investors. A $2 trillion valuation would be roughly double the $965 billion post-money mark Anthropic set in May. Investors would be paying that price for Class A shares in a company that says in writing it may put its mission ahead of their returns. Dual-class structures are common in tech; one explicitly tied to AI safety is new.

## What to Watch

Reuters says the listing is likely to come after the U.S. midterm elections in November, so the public S-1 and its pricing range are the next milestones. Watch for three things when those arrive. The first is how the filing schedules the $518 billion in obligations year by year. The second is whether 2026 quarterly figures show compute costs growing more slowly than revenue. The third is how much voting power Class A holders actually get relative to the Founder LLC and the company's Long-Term Benefit Trust. Together, those details will decide whether the $2 trillion figure holds up once the shares start trading.
