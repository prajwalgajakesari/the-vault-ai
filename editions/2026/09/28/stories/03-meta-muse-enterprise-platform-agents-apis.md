# Meta Takes Muse to Work, Launching an Enterprise Platform with Agents and APIs for Businesses

Three weeks after Muse turned Meta from an AI also-ran into the owner of the top free app on the U.S. iPhone charts, Mark Zuckerberg is trying to turn that consumer hit into a corporate revenue line. On Monday, Meta launched Meta Enterprise Platform, a new business unit that will package the Muse agent, Meta Business Agent, the Muse API and the Muse Code coding agent for companies and developers. To run it, Meta hired Chirantan "CJ" Desai away from his job as CEO of MongoDB.

"Today we are starting the next major pillar of our business, Meta Enterprise Platform, to help businesses use AI to grow and transform in new ways as well," Zuckerberg wrote in the announcement. He framed the push around assets he said "few other companies have": advanced models, leading agents, large-scale infrastructure, and years of working with the hundreds of millions of businesses that already reach customers on Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Messenger.

## What Meta is actually selling

The launch is more of a reorganization and a statement of intent than a finished product catalog. Meta did not publish enterprise pricing, and it did not name any launch customers or new partners. What it did describe is a stack that has been assembled quickly over the past five months. Muse Spark, the model underneath everything, debuted in April and has been updated several times; the September release, Muse Spark 1.3, is aimed at longer tasks, coding and tool use. Meta says that in its own engineers' comparisons the new version used roughly 20% fewer tool calls and 25% fewer tokens than its predecessor, according to The Neuron.

Muse Code, the coding agent built on that model, left beta at Meta's Connect conference and is now available on Windows. The Muse API gives developers direct access to the models, and Meta says its Model API is generally available globally. The Neuron reports that Muse Spark 1.3 is also offered through Oracle Cloud AI Platform, with Google Cloud availability in private preview, giving enterprises a way to try Meta's models inside clouds they already use.

The most mature piece is Meta Business Agent, which handles customer questions, product recommendations, appointment booking and sales across Meta's messaging apps. Meta said in June that more than one million businesses were already using a Business Agent on WhatsApp and Messenger, Techloy notes. That installed base, rather than Muse itself, may be Meta's strongest wedge into business customers.

Desai, who reports directly to Zuckerberg, previously led product and engineering at Cloudflare and spent nearly eight years at ServiceNow, including as president and COO. "Meta Enterprise Platform will focus on turning its AI stack into products and services that companies can deploy for their own businesses," he said in a statement, adding that "security and privacy are built into Meta's enterprise products from the outset." The hire came at a cost to his former employer: MongoDB shares fell more than 17% on news of his sudden departure, TechCrunch reported, and the company reinstated Dev Ittycheria as interim CEO.

## The privacy question follows Muse into the office

Desai's emphasis on privacy is not incidental. Meta's pitch to businesses depends on a trust reset it only began in earnest this summer. In an August manifesto, Zuckerberg wrote that "people should have a fully private mode for personal agents where even Meta or any other service provider cannot see or grant access to your information." With Muse, users can choose which apps it connects to and opt out of having their interactions train Meta's models, and Muse data will not feed Meta's ad systems. A confidential virtual machine option, encrypted with a key held only by the user, has been promised but has not shipped.

Axios chief technology correspondent Ina Fried, a longtime skeptic of Meta's data practices, wrote on Monday that she has started "tentatively" using Muse after switching off model training, while waiting for the confidential mode. Her verdict was cautious: "Trust, but verify." Default settings still allow Muse interactions to be used for training, Axios reported last week, and the protections do not extend to Meta AI inside Facebook or Instagram.

The business model also differs from Meta's ad machine. Muse has $20 and $100 monthly tiers for heavy users, but Meta expects most usage to be free, subsidized by a cut of transactions Muse completes. Alexandr Wang, Meta's chief AI officer, has described the agent as a kind of "second mind" that plans and executes on a user's behalf.

## Why It Matters

Enterprise AI is where the durable revenue is, and Meta has until now been the only frontier lab without a real business-software story. Microsoft sells Copilot and its agents through existing Microsoft 365 contracts, Google bundles Gemini into Workspace and Google Cloud, and OpenAI has built a fast-growing ChatGPT Enterprise and API business. Meta's advantage is different: it already has commercial relationships with millions of advertisers and small businesses that live in WhatsApp and Messenger, and a consumer product that people are actually choosing over ChatGPT. Muse drew more than 2.5 million downloads after its September 8 launch, according to Reuters figures cited by Techloy.

The weakness is everything enterprises buy besides the model: compliance certifications, admin controls, data residency, contractual privacy guarantees and a support organization. Meta has none of those at the scale of Microsoft or Google, which is exactly the gap a veteran of ServiceNow and MongoDB was hired to close. Without published pricing or reference customers, the platform is still a promise.

## What to Watch

The immediate test is competitive. OpenAI is reportedly preparing a persistent, always-on assistant of its own, widely described as a response to Muse, with some reports pointing to an announcement as soon as this week. Beyond that, watch for Meta's first named enterprise customers, a published price list for the Muse API and agents, general availability on Google Cloud, and, above all, whether the user-keyed confidential VM actually ships. For corporate buyers, that may matter more than any benchmark.
